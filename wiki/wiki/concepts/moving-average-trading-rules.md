# Moving-average and technical trading rules

**Moving-average and technical trading rules** are rule-based FX trading signals (e.g. moving-average crossovers, filter rules, dynamic technical rules) documented to be profitable in currency markets though useless in equities; treated here as the incumbent FX momentum-style benchmark the combination strategy beats.

## Overview

Serban (2010) notes a longstanding asymmetry between markets: technical trading rules were found "completely useless" in the stock market after Fama and Blume (1966), yet profitable in FX (Sweeney 1986; Szakmary and Mathur 1997; LeBaron 1999), suggesting fundamental differences between the two markets. The paper benchmarks its [[concepts/mean-reversion-momentum-combination-strategy|combination strategy]] against these rules — for example LeBaron's (1999) dynamic trading rule delivers annualized Sharpe ratios of about 0.67–0.96 — and finds the combination strategy usually outperforms them. It also relates to Okunev and White's (2003) moving-average-based "simple momentum" FX strategy, which the combination approach can match or exceed despite originating in equities.
