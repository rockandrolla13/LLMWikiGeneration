# Forward premium puzzle

**Forward premium puzzle** is the empirical regularity (Fama 1984) that forward exchange rates are biased predictors of future spot rates — high-interest (forward-discount) currencies tend to appreciate rather than depreciate — implying a rejection of UIP and the existence of a predictable currency risk premium.

## Overview

Serban (2010) takes the "forward puzzle" as the starting point of the paper: whereas [[concepts/uncovered-interest-parity|UIP]] predicts that currencies at a forward premium should appreciate, the data show the opposite, so forward rates systematically mispredict spot rates. This rejection of UIP is what creates exploitable structure in the deviations from parity. The puzzle motivates treating UIP deviations as a return series with a predictable component, connecting the FX evidence to the broader literature on currency [[concepts/risk-premia|risk premia]] and to overshooting/overreaction explanations of exchange-rate behavior.
