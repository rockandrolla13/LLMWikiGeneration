# Sentiment-persistence factor autocorrelation

**Sentiment-persistence factor autocorrelation** is a mechanism from the Kozak-Nagel-Santosh model in which persistent sentiment-investor demand aligned with common factor covariances generates factor momentum (rather than reversal) when the AR(1) persistence of sentiment exceeds a threshold near the inverse risk-free rate. Arbitrageurs do not fully neutralize the predictability because doing so would expose them to factor risk, and momentum is predicted to concentrate in high-eigenvalue factors.

## Overview

Ehsani and Linnainmaa interpret their evidence through the Kozak, Nagel, and Santosh (KNS) sentiment framework. When non-fundamental demand shocks are sufficiently persistent, prices trend rather than mean-revert, producing positive factor autocorrelation. Because sentiment demand loads on common covariance structure, the resulting predictability is greatest for [[concepts/high-eigenvalue-factor-momentum|high-eigenvalue factors]], which arbitrageurs cannot fully offset without bearing systematic factor risk. This gives the paper a theoretically grounded account of why factor momentum exists and why it concentrates where it does, while leaving open a clean separation of risk-based versus mispricing-based explanations.
