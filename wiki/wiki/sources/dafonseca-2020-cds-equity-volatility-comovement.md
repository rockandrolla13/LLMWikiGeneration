# Co-Movement of CDS Spreads, Equity Returns and Volatility

**Authors:** José Da Fonseca (Auckland University of Technology; PRISM Sorbonne), Katrin Gottschalk (AUT)

**Year:** 2020 · **Venue:** International Review of Finance 20(3), 551–579 · DOI 10.1111/irfi.12237

## Summary

Analyses how CDS, equity and volatility markets move together in **four Asia-Pacific countries, 2007–2010**, at both firm and index level. Three pieces of analysis.

## Findings

**Lead-lag, by vector autoregression.** At the **firm level**, equity returns lead changes in CDS spreads and realized volatility. At the **index level** the linkages are less clear-cut — the direction that holds for single names does not survive aggregation.

**Spillovers, by Diebold-Yilmaz (2014).** Realized volatility is the main transmitter of cross-market volatility spillovers at firm level; implied volatility takes that role at index level.

**Determinants.** Structural factors are tested, and realized volatility of equity returns is confirmed as a determinant of CDS spreads.

## Why It Belongs Here

The theoretical anchor is Merton (1974): credit risk, equity and volatility are three views of the same firm value process. See [[concepts/merton-model|Merton Model]] and [[concepts/structural-models|Structural Models]].

Its contribution to this cluster is the **firm-versus-index split**. The other papers here work cross-sectionally on single names; this one shows the relationship between volatility and credit is clean at firm level and muddy at index level. Anything that aggregates to an index — a credit ETF, a CDS index hedge — is operating where the linkage is weakest.

## Open Questions

- 2007–2010 is a crisis window. Does the firm-level lead-lag hold in calm markets?
- Why does the transmitter switch from realized to implied volatility between firm and index level?
