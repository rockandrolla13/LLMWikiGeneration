# 2020 in Review

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** March 4, 2021 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2020_in_review.pdf)

## Summary

Marks's annual client review of 2020, written in March 2021. He catalogues the year's extremes: the worst pandemic in a century, a 32.9% annualised GDP drop followed by a 33.4% gain, a 33.9% S&P 500 crash in 32 days and a full recovery within five months, and record bond issuance. He attributes the gap between a sick economy and strong markets to the Fed and Treasury, and notes that the buying opportunity was unusually brief. He reports Oaktree's record deployment and fundraising (including Opportunities Fund XI at $14.5 billion), profiles the Power Opportunities funds, and covers diversity, ESG and pandemic operations. The longest section weighs bull and bear cases for 2021. He judges most assets to be in a gray area on the high side of fair, names rising interest rates and inflation as the main risk, and recommends a normal risk posture with a modest defensive bias: underweight long bonds, consider inflation beneficiaries.

## Key Ideas

1. Aggressive Fed and Treasury action explains why markets boomed in a pandemic recession, and why the 2020 buying opportunity lasted roughly a month.
2. Oaktree did not forecast the recovery. It was defensive while markets looked vulnerable and turned aggressive when price declines made defensiveness inappropriate.
3. Perceived clarity about the future is often illusory. Macro forecasts mostly extrapolate, and only rare, correct calls of major deviation are profitable.
4. With rates near zero after a 40-year decline, rates can rise but not fall, a negatively asymmetrical proposition for asset prices.
5. Valuations must be read in context: the S&P 500's 4.5% earnings yield over a 1.4% 10-year gives a ~310bp equity risk premium, in line with the 20-year average.
6. Speculative signals were present: meme stocks, heavy retail and options buying, weak bond covenants, the Buffett Indicator above its prior high, and unprofitable IPOs.
7. Even though we can't predict, we should prepare: underweight bonds much above ten years and consider inflation beneficiaries such as floating-rate debt, real estate with rising rents and firms with pricing power.
8. Disciplined fundraising protects returns: a niche that earns great returns with little risk cannot be infinitely scalable (Power Fund II's 59% net IRR, next fund still $1 billion).

## Market Context

Early March 2021: vaccines rolling out, the Fed at zero rates and buying bonds after growing its balance sheet by $2.7 trillion, a proposed $1.9 trillion Biden relief package, the 10-year Treasury yield up to 1.40% from 0.52% in August 2020, the S&P 500 at about 22x forward earnings, and signs of speculation (meme stocks, retail options buying, IPO pops). Larry Summers had warned of inflation risk from the stimulus.

## Notable Claims

- S&P 500 fell 33.9% in 32 days, then regained the high in under five months.
- Record 2020 high yield issuance of $450 billion.
- Oaktree closed-end funds deployed nearly $17 billion, a record.
- Opps XI believed to be the largest distressed debt fund ever, at $14.5 billion.
- Implied equity risk premium of 310bp versus a 20-year average of 300bp.

## Quotes

> We simply favored defensiveness when we considered the markets vulnerable and then turned aggressive when price declines rendered defensiveness no longer appropriate.

> With arguments on both sides, I feel the prices of most assets are in a gray area – certainly not low, mostly on the high side of fair, but not so high as to be unreasonable.

> But the inescapable truth of investing remains unchanged: there is no magic answer, no solution (other than superior skill) that will enable an investor to earn a high return safely and dependably.
