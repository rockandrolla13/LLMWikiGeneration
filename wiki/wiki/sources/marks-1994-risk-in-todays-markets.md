# Risk in Today's Markets

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** February 17, 1994 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1994-02-17-risk-in-todays-markets.pdf)

## Summary

Written while Marks was still at TCW, this short client memo argues that two trends -- thirty-year-low interest rates and the strong 1991-93 returns earned by risk-takers -- have pushed investors to accept more risk across the whole system, often without realising it, at a time when valuations are already high. He describes a 'food chain' in which each class of investor moves one step out on the risk curve, uses the hedge fund boom as an illustration of symmetric, two-edged risk, and warns that low rates which inflate valuations can cut the other way. He does not preach risk avoidance; instead he urges clients to seek returns that are more than proportionate to risk, through inefficient markets, capital preservation, specialisation, careful analysis and senior securities.

## Key Ideas

1. Low rates plus recent high returns for risk-takers create an environment where risk-taking is actively encouraged.
2. Markets usually run on fear OR greed, not both; in early 1994 greed was elevated and fear was scarce.
3. A risk 'food chain': money-market investors move to bonds, bond investors extend maturity and drop in quality, bond investors go to equities, equity investors to hedge funds, domestic to foreign, international to emerging markets.
4. Most investments are symmetric: a strategy that makes a lot when things go well will usually lose a lot when they go badly, unless markets are very inefficient.
5. Low interest rates make low earnings and dividend yields look tolerable and raise model valuations, but that effect can reverse.
6. No action is wise or foolish in itself; it depends on timing, price paid and how others are behaving.
7. Oaktree-style cornerstones: exploit inefficient markets, preserve capital, refuse maximum return at maximum risk, specialise, analyse carefully, prefer senior securities.

## Market Context

Early 1994: interest rates at a 30-year low, strong returns across securities from 1991 to 1993, heavy mutual fund inflows (mostly to foreign funds), rising margin debt, a hedge fund boom, and the Fed's first small rate rise on February 4 triggering a 96-point Dow drop. Marks's high yield group was at TCW and had avoided much of the 1989-90 high yield damage.

## Notable Claims

- A small Fed-mandated rate rise on February 4 pushed the Dow down 96 points, suggesting unpreparedness for bad news.
- A fund group said it was taking in $100 million a day, 90% for foreign funds.
- Forbes counted 800 hedge funds.
- Some hedge funds made 70%+ when the average stock or bond returned 10-15%, via leveraged macro bets.
- Interest rates were at the lowest levels of the last thirty years.

## Quotes

> It is often said that the market runs on fear and greed, but I believe it usually runs on fear or greed

> almost every sword is two-edged, and he who lives by a risky strategy may die by it.

> What the wise man does in the beginning, the fool does in the end.
