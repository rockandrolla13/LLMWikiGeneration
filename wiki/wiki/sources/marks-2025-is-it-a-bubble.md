# Is It a Bubble?

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** December 9, 2025 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/is-it-a-bubble.pdf)

## Summary

Prompted by client questions in Asia and the Middle East, Marks asks whether artificial intelligence is in a bubble, separating a bubble in companies' behaviour from one in investors' behaviour. He describes the regular progression of bubbles and the role of newness in removing historical limits on imagination. Using Hobart and Huber's split between 'mean-reversion' and 'inflection' bubbles and Carlota Perez's installation and deployment phases, he argues that technology bubbles speed up progress but still destroy investors' wealth. He lists the uncertainties: who wins, whether AI earns profits for vendors or users, circular deals, the useful life of chips, $1-2 billion seed rounds, and an undefined end state. He focuses on the growing use of debt: hyperscaler 30-year bonds at 100 bp or less over Treasurys, SPVs, and vendor financing that hint at a Minsky moment. His conclusion: enthusiasm is clearly high, whether it is irrational cannot yet be known, and a moderate, selective position is best. A postscript voices deep worry about AI-driven job losses.

## Key Ideas

1. Bubbles follow a regular progression: novelty, early gains, envy and FOMO, buying without regard to price, then pain.
2. Newness removes history's restraint on imagination, letting 'limitless' futures justify valuations beyond past norms.
3. Inflection bubbles (railroads, internet) leave the world changed but still destroy wealth; the key is not to be the investor who funds progress at a loss.
4. 'Lottery-ticket thinking' about trillion-dollar payoffs overrides reasonable probability estimates.
5. Circular deals, SPVs and vendor financing echo the telecom/fiber bust and Enron.
6. Debt suits ventures with uncertain outcomes but not ones whose outcome is pure conjecture; winner-take-most technologies are better played via equity than diversified debt.
7. 'This time it's different' beliefs are what make it not different.
8. Neither go all-in nor stay all-out; take a moderate, selective, prudent position.

## Market Context

December 2025: AI stocks drove most S&P 500 gains (Fortune: 75% of gains, 80% of profits, 90% of capex); hyperscalers were moving from cash-funded to debt-funded data-centre build-outs, including 30-year bonds and off-balance-sheet SPVs; AI startups raised multi-billion-dollar seed rounds (Thinking Machines, SSI); Nvidia had briefly hit $5 trillion; Chinese open models were gaining download share.

## Notable Claims

- Nvidia rose from a ~$626m value at its 1999 IPO to briefly $5tn, about 8,000x.
- OpenAI has made $1.4tn of investment commitments without having turned a profit.
- Meta and Alphabet 30-year AI bonds yield 100 bp or less over Treasurys.
- Goldman estimates 15% of Nvidia's next-year sales come from circular deals.
- Biggest AI spenders had about $350bn of cash versus ~$5tn of estimated build-out cost.

> Of course, if you can't identify the pool of companies from which the winner will emerge, the difference between debt and equity is irrelevant – you're a zero either way.

— Bob O'Leary, Oaktree's co-CEO, quoted by Marks
