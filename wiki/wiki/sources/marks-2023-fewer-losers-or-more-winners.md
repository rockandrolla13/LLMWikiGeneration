# Fewer Losers, or More Winners?

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** September 12, 2023 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/fewer-losers-more-winner.pdf)

## Summary

Marks revisits Oaktree's motto, 'If we avoid the losers, the winners will take care of themselves', which grew out of his first memo in October 1990 and a General Mills pension fund whose steady second-quartile years added up to a fourth-percentile record. He traces the idea to Graham and Dodd's description of fixed-income investing as 'a negative art' and to Jesse Livermore, then explains why it fits bonds but not 'aspirational' strategies like distressed debt that need winners. He distinguishes risk control from risk avoidance, uses Wimbledon 2023 statistics (Medvedev vs Eubanks, Alcaraz vs Djokovic) to show that the balance of winners to losers decides outcomes, and shows that index concentration in the 'magnificent seven' and Apple forces active managers to hold winners they are tempted to sell. He closes with his risk-return graph of widening probability distributions and defines alpha as the skill to make those distributions asymmetric.

## Key Ideas

1. Consistency beats occasional brilliance: a fund never above the 27th or below the 47th percentile ranked in the 4th percentile over 14 years.
2. Bond investing is a 'negative art' (Graham and Dodd): with a fixed coupon, performance comes from excluding defaulters, not picking winners.
3. Strategies aiming above bond returns, such as distressed debt, must find or create winners. Risk control remains Oaktree's first tenet.
4. Risk control is not risk avoidance. Avoiding risk usually means avoiding return. Intelligent risk is known, analysable, diversifiable and well paid.
5. There is a 'risk of taking too little risk': 'If you don't experience any defaults, you're probably not taking enough credit risk' (Sheldon Stone).
6. In tennis and investing, outcomes depend on the ratio of winners to losers. Style alone never determines outcome; execution matters.
7. Index concentration (magnificent seven, Apple up almost 500x since 2003) means active investors who trim winners for profit-taking tend to lag.
8. Higher risk widens the range of outcomes and worsens the bad ones. Alpha is the skill to skew that distribution so the downside is smaller than the upside.

## Market Context

September 2023: the 'magnificent seven' tech stocks (Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Nvidia, Tesla, Meta) accounted for most or all of the year's index gains, with the other 493 S&P 500 companies flat in aggregate as of June (FT). Passive investing had taken a large share of equity capital. Marks mentions recent health issues and well-wishers.

## Notable Claims

- General Mills pension fund's second-quartile years produced a 4th-percentile 14-year record.
- Eubanks hit 74 winners but 55 unforced errors against Medvedev's 13.
- The big three won 87% of Grand Slams in the 19 years to Wimbledon 2023.
- Apple rose almost 500x from a split-adjusted $0.37 in 2003 to about $180.
- Munger told Marks most of his wealth came from only four winners.

## Quotes

> If we avoid the losers, the winners will take care of themselves.

> There’s such a thing as the risk of taking too little risk.

> Style alone never determines outcome; it’s a matter of style plus execution.
