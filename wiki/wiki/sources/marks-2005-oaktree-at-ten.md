# Oaktree at Ten

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** April 11, 2005 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2005-04-11-oaktree-at-ten.pdf)

## Summary

A tenth-anniversary review of Oaktree, which opened on April 10, 1995. Marks explains that the firm was built on an investment philosophy and business principles rather than a business plan, with the motto that avoiding losers lets the winners take care of themselves. He reports the client base, a record of making money for clients every year, profitable closed-end funds, deliberate limits on assets, the addition of five new strategies and international offices since 1998, low senior staff turnover, and gradual broadening of ownership to employees and a small stake sold to long-term clients. The goal for the next decade is more of the same: performance that is consistently good or great, with no disasters.

## Key Ideas

1. Oaktree's founding motto: if we avoid the losers, the winners will take care of themselves.
2. The aim is to match the market in good times and do markedly better in bad times, eliminating disasters so every year is good or great.
3. Oaktree works only in inefficient markets and does not try to gain an edge from macro forecasts or market timing.
4. Limiting assets under management was among the best decisions; high yield turned away about $14 billion of new assets since November 1998.
5. New strategies must meet three criteria: an inefficient market, a way to exploit it with risk under control, and capable people.
6. Business principles: proprietary research, conflicts resolved for the client, aligned compensation, truthful communication including bad news.
7. Ownership was shared with employees (about 25%) and about 6% sold to seven long-term clients, without ceding control.

## Market Context

Written in April 2005 as a corporate anniversary letter rather than a market commentary. Oaktree had about $28 billion and roughly 300 people. Marks alludes to recent industry scandals where asset gathering was put ahead of client performance.

## Notable Claims

- Founding date of Oaktree.
- All 24 closed-end funds from 1988-2003 were profitable.
- Legacy strategies grew from about $6-7 billion to $22.5 billion.
- Oaktree turned away $14 billion of high yield assets.
- Clients include 23 of the 25 largest distressed-debt pension investors.

## Quotes

> if we avoid the losers, the winners will take care of themselves.

> it’s our goal to eliminate disasters, so that every year is either good or great.

> In short, we like the idea of working together, but not of working for someone else.
