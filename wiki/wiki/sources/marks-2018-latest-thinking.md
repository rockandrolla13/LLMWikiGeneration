# Latest Thinking

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** January 23, 2018 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/latest-thinking.pdf)

## Summary

Marks gives a balanced view of markets in early 2018 and then analyses the new US tax law. On markets, he lists the positives (a long recovery, worldwide growth, a pro-business administration, low unemployment and inflation) and the negatives (record or near-record valuations, low prospective returns, pro-risk behaviour, FOMO and unusually low volatility). He concludes the easy money has been made and favours the cautious end of the spectrum while staying invested under Oaktree's 'move forward, but with caution' mantra, refusing to bet on a possible melt-up. On taxes, he judges the law a stimulative tax cut rather than a reform, odd while the Fed is tightening, likely to raise deficits and debt, and harmful to high-tax states through the $10,000 SALT cap, which may drive high earners out of states like New York.

## Key Ideas

1. Never say 'get out' or 'it's time': markets rarely reach extremes that justify black-or-white calls.
2. Fundamentals are strong (103-month recovery, synchronised global growth, 4.1% unemployment) but prices already reflect them.
3. Most valuation measures were the richest ever or among the highest in history; investing requires believing 'it's different this time'.
4. Low prospective returns push investors into pro-risk behaviour and FOMO; 2017 had no S&P 500 drawdown above 3%.
5. Oaktree's stance: 'move forward, but with caution', fully invested in a cautious portfolio, without timing a possible melt-up.
6. A decision is wrong if it was wrong when made, not because it failed to work out; being early is not being wrong.
7. The tax law is a pro-cyclical fiscal stimulus that adds over $1 trillion to deficits and is likely a short-term positive and long-term negative.
8. The $10,000 SALT cap raises the incentive for high earners to leave high-tax states, risking a negative spiral for states like New York.

## Market Context

January 2018, about a year into the Trump administration and just after passage of the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017. The US recovery was in its ninth year, unemployment was 4.1%, the Fed was raising rates and reversing QE, the VIX was very low, and there was talk of a late-bull-market melt-up. Marks had just submitted the final draft of his book on cycles, due in October.

## Notable Claims

- The recovery that began in 2009 was 103 months old.
- 2017 had no S&P 500 drawdown above 3%.
- S&P 500 roughly quadrupled from the 2009 low.
- Corporate tax rate cut from 35% to 21%.
- $2.8 trillion of foreign profits stranded overseas.
