# Yet Again?

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** September 7, 2017 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/yet-again.pdf)

## Summary

A follow-up to 'There They Go Again . . . Again' (July 26, 2017), which drew the most response of any memo in 28 years. Marks answers critics. He never says 'get out' or 'it's time'; the key word is 'calibrate' risk along an aggressive-defensive continuum according to price relative to value. Holding past one's sell points signals that fear of missing out has displaced discipline. He also rejects the view that caution should be constant, arguing that cycle adjustment, based on observations of the present rather than forecasts, can add value, as Oaktree's shifts in 1990-91, 2001-02, 2005-06 and 2008 showed. On the FAANGs, his point was that anointing a group of super-stocks signals a bull market. On Bitcoin he concedes that it may function as a currency (fiat money also lacks intrinsic backing), but he still views its price as a speculative bubble driven by 'lottery-ticket thinking'. He raises concerns that passive investing, at roughly 35% of US equities, distorts prices in favour of index constituents. He calls the market 'high and therefore risky', not a bubble. For a low-return world he recommends mostly investing as usual with lower return expectations, added caution, and an emphasis on alpha niches.

## Key Ideas

1. Investing isn't in-or-out; calibrate the amount invested, allocation and riskiness along a continuum from aggressive to defensive.
2. 'It's not what's going on; it's how it's priced': be aggressive when value is cheap and pull back when it's expensive.
3. Two routes to outperformance are selection and cycle adjustment; saying 'there is no better or worse time' gives up on the second.
4. Knowing where we stand needs only observations of the present (valuations, ease of risky financing, psychology), not forecasts; it shifts the odds but is not always right, and certainly not right away.
5. Anointing a group of super-stocks (FAANGs) is itself a sign of a bull market.
6. Bitcoin may qualify as a currency, but whether today's price is right is a separate question; 'trading sardines' and lottery-ticket thinking mark a speculative bubble.
7. Reflexivity and passive flows: index buying regardless of price may make index stocks expensive relative to non-index stocks, creating openings for active managers.
8. In a low-return world, the options are limited; Marks would invest as usual with lower expectations, added caution and an emphasis on 'alpha markets' ('move forward, but with caution').

## Market Context

September 2017, eight-plus years into the post-GFC bull market, with near-zero risk-free rates, elevated valuations, FAANG enthusiasm, a sharp Bitcoin rally (about $4,600) and fast growth of passive and ETF investing. Marks saw no recession imminent and no subprime-like analogue.

## Notable Claims

- Roughly 35% of US equity investing was estimated to be passive.
- Bitcoin was up more than 350% year to date and 3,900% over three years, at about $4,600.
- Bitcoin supply is capped to grow from 16 million to 21 million by 2140.
- More than $1 billion a day was flowing to passive managers who buy regardless of price.
- Fleeing from high yield (5-6%) to Treasurys (1-2%) would cost about 4% a year.

## Quotes

> I told Bloomberg, “Investing is not black or white, in or out, risky or safe.”

> “Oh,” says Jim, “these aren’t eating sardines; these are trading sardines.”

— a joke from Marks's father, retold by Marks

> It’s time for caution, as I wrote in the memo, not a full-scale exodus.
