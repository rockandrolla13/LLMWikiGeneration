# What Worries Me

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** August 28, 2008 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2008-08-28-what-worries-me.pdf)

## Summary

An unusual macro and societal memo in which Marks sets aside investment cycles to list his long-term worries about America. He argues that national preeminence is cyclical and that the U.S. advantages behind 'The American Century' are eroding: immigration is a weaker draw, U.S. wages stay high only while barriers last, the country makes less and consumes more, and consumers and government both spend more than they earn and lean on foreign lenders. He covers retirement shortfalls, healthcare trade-offs, growing inequality, cheap-energy habits, and a huge transfer of wealth to oil producers. Political short-termism blocks solutions. Higher top tax rates and trade barriers will not work, and short-term pain such as high oil prices may be part of the cure. A post-script from Richard Masson offers a more optimistic view.

## Key Ideas

1. National preeminence is cyclical; leaders overextend and underdogs (China, India, Russia, Brazil) catch up.
2. Geographic wage gaps depend on barriers to resource mobility; as barriers fall, U.S. earnings supremacy is hard to sustain.
3. An economy that makes little and imports much faces questions about what it will sell to fund its purchases.
4. Consumers and government both run persistent deficits; dependence on foreign lenders constrains rates and pressures the dollar.
5. A century of cheap oil encouraged profligate use; only high prices cut demand and raise supply, so politicians' price fixes backfire.
6. Roughly $1 trillion moves to oil producers per $1 of oil price over a hundred years, enough to 'own the world'.
7. Political short-termism blocks action on Social Security, healthcare, deficits and energy.
8. Simple fixes like higher top tax rates and trade barriers distort incentives and reduce overall welfare.

## Market Context

Late August 2008, weeks before the Lehman bankruptcy: Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac had just been rescued, subprime and CDO losses had damaged U.S. financial exports, oil had hit $148 earlier in the year and then fallen about 20% in six weeks, sovereign wealth funds were recapitalising banks, and a U.S. presidential election campaign was under way.

## Notable Claims

- Congress raised the debt ceiling from $9.8tn to $10.6tn with the Fannie/Freddie rescue.
- Oil went from $4 in 1964 to $92 in 2007 and $148 in 2008.
- Oil at $120 implies about $120tn to producers, versus ~$47tn world stock market value.
- Americans drove 12.2 billion (5%) fewer miles in June 2008 than a year earlier.
- Friedman: U.S. national savings fell from 6% to 1% of GDP since 2000.

## Quotes

> National preeminence – like most other things – is cyclical, not permanent.

> But as long as gasoline costs much less than Snapple or Evian water, resources will be misallocated and we won't see real progress.

> And we need officeholders capable of imagining outcomes worse than losing an election.
