# How's the Market?

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** April 15, 1999 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1999-04-15-hows-the-market.pdf)

## Summary

A short memo that uses one Wall Street Journal article about an ordinary record-setting day, April 5, 1999, to diagnose the late-1990s bull market. Marks points to narrow breadth, with a handful of large-cap and internet stocks driving index gains while most stocks lagged. He highlights indifference to valuation, the tendency of bulls and the media to read every data point favourably, contradictory strategist advice, and statements that make no sense. He admits value investors like himself have been early in calling excess and quotes the line that being too far ahead of your time looks the same as being wrong. A postscript contrasts the internet darlings with Computer Outsourcing Services, a growing company whose stock lagged badly.

## Key Ideas

1. Index-level gains hid narrow participation: a few stocks drove the S&P 500 while most lost money.
2. Investors setting prices in 1999 ignored valuation measures such as P/E, EBITDA multiples and dividend yield.
3. In a bull market, the same economic data gets interpreted positively that would be read negatively in other times.
4. Internet-company valuations such as Priceline's had become detached from revenues and losses.
5. Being early in calling a bubble is costly and indistinguishable from being wrong in the short run.
6. Solid growth companies without '.com' in their name were neglected, with real business costs such as hiring problems.

## Market Context

April 1999, late in the dot-com bull market. The Dow set records, internet stocks such as Yahoo, Amazon and America Online soared, and Priceline had just gone public. Breadth was narrow and second-tier stocks declined.

## Notable Claims

- 18 stocks produced all of the S&P 500's Q1 1999 rise.
- Most S&P stocks lost money and the Russell 2000 fell.
- Priceline was valued above three airlines combined.
- More new lows than new highs on a record day.
- Computer Outsourcing Services grew but its stock lagged.

## Quotes

> being too far ahead of your time is indistinguishable from being wrong.

> As for me, I'm certain one of them will be proved right.

> Lots of sound and fury, signifying nothing.
