# Etorre's Wisdom

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** September 4, 2002 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2002-09-04-etorres-wisdom.pdf)

## Summary

Prompted by his son asking why he drives in the slow lane, Marks uses 'Etorre's Observation' (the other line moves faster) as a metaphor for efficient markets. When everyone switches to the fast lane it becomes the slow lane, just as investors crowding into the best performers bid up prices and lower future returns while the abandoned laggards become cheap. Lane-jumpers and market timers achieve close to zero expected gain at higher cost and risk, and investors in hot funds often do worse than the funds themselves. The way to do better is to seek inefficiency: go where others will not, as Oaktree does in junk bonds, bankruptcies, distressed debt and other unloved niches.

## Key Ideas

1. A crowded highway is a metaphor for an efficient market: collective switching equalises lane speeds and erases excess returns.
2. Past returns change investor behaviour, and that behaviour changes future returns; markets are dynamic, unlike a golf course.
3. Momentum investors in the 1999 tech and telecom bubble extrapolated outperformance forever and could not exit at the peak.
4. Anything can work for a while, but nothing works forever; style leadership rotates.
5. The expected value of lane changing (market timing, short-term trading) is near zero and worse after costs and risk.
6. Investors in volatile hot funds often earn less than the fund because money arrives near the top and the fund is larger when it falls.
7. Superior risk-adjusted returns are found outside the mainstream, in things others find frightening, unseemly or ignored.

## Market Context

Written in September 2002, after the collapse of the 1999-2000 tech and telecom bubble. Marks looks back at momentum investors who extrapolated tech outperformance and sold 'slow lane' value stocks.

## Notable Claims

- Crowding into the fast lane makes it slow.
- Investor returns in volatile funds lag the fund's own returns.
- Some managers compound at 25% for 25 years, then lose 50% on a large asset base.
- Oaktree targets frightening or unseemly assets.
- Superior returns without commensurate risk exist outside the mainstream.

## Quotes

> The other line moves faster.

> anything can work for a while, but nothing can work forever.

> A shortcut that everyone knows about is an absolute oxymoron
