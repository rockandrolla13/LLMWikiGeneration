# Information Arrival as a Stochastic Clock for Intraday Trading

## Summary

The paper asks how information arrival governs the pace of intraday trading, seeking a single framework that jointly explains the well known bathtub-shaped intraday pattern in trading intensity, volume, realized volatility, and execution risk, rather than treating deterministic seasonality, self-exciting order flow, and news-driven activity as separate phenomena.

It builds a compound Hawkes process for trade-arrival intensity from three additive pieces: a deterministic bathtub-shaped baseline reflecting the open/close liquidity cycle, a linear self-exciting trade-feedback term built from a decaying accumulation of past trade sizes, and a news-pressure term that squares each signed news mark before it enters the intensity, so equal-magnitude favorable and unfavorable news raise trading intensity identically even though the same signed mark enters expected price changes linearly, with opposite sign. Parameters can in principle be fit by maximum likelihood from trade timestamps and a timestamped news feed; the paper derives a diffusive limit for the trade-time component of log price under a 'clock law of large numbers,' obtains a closed-form VWAP execution profile, and illustrates the mechanics with one simulated trading session rather than a historical calibration.

The central theoretical result is that the trade-time log-price martingale's limiting variance is proportional to average trading intensity, and that the model imposes an odd-function (directional) price response to news alongside an even-function (magnitude-only) response in trading activity, volume, and residual variance. Equal-and-opposite news surprises should therefore look identical in activity and volatility terms while having opposite effects on expected price direction.

What is new is the specific squared-mark restriction linking one signed news variable to both an odd price effect and an even activity effect inside a single Hawkes intensity, together with the resulting joint, directly testable predictions it proposes for future estimation: a variance-intensity scaling regression, a VWAP curve fit, and a test that news sign should not predict the incremental trading-intensity response once magnitude is controlled for.

## Clock and Sampling

**Time change: a stochastic clock used as a modelling device.**

See [[concepts/stochastic-time-change|Stochastic Time Change]].

The model treats trading activity as governed by an underlying stochastic clock: a Hawkes trading-intensity process built from a deterministic bathtub-shaped intraday baseline, a linear self-exciting trade-feedback term, and a squared-mark news-pressure term. Prices are analyzed in 'trade time' subordinated to this clock; the paper proves that, as the number of trading sessions grows, the trade-time component of the midpoint log price converges to a Brownian motion whose variance is proportional to average trading intensity. There is no single fixed prediction horizon; instead the paper derives closed-form intraday profiles (intensity, volume, VWAP participation) as functions of time of day and news history.

## Data

- **Asset class:** Simulated data
- **Instruments:** No real asset is used; the paper illustrates the model with a single simulated day of trade and news arrivals, and suggests future parameter estimation using standard transaction-level and news-feed data.
- **Venue:** not stated
- **Period:** One simulated 390-minute trading session with a premarket burn-in period before the open.
- **Granularity:** Simulated event-time trade and news arrivals generated by thinning the fitted intensity process; a fine deterministic grid is used only to plot the resulting paths, not to step the simulation.

## Features and Measures

- **Bathtub-shaped baseline intensity.** A deterministic component of trading intensity that is elevated near the open and close and lower at midday, built from a constant floor plus a decaying opening amplitude and a growing closing amplitude.
- **Marked linear trade-feedback state.** A decaying, self-exciting accumulation of past trade sizes, each mapped through a size-to-impact function, that contributes linearly to current trading intensity and captures endogenous order-flow clustering.
- **Squared-mark news-pressure state.** A decaying accumulation of squared signed news scores; each news arrival adds its squared mark, so favorable and unfavorable news of the same magnitude add identically to trading intensity even though they move prices in opposite directions.
- **Model-implied VWAP execution profile.** A closed-form expected intraday participation schedule for executing a parent order, derived directly from the same three-component trading-intensity model rather than from a historical volume curve.

## Method

Trade arrivals are modeled as a marked Hawkes process whose intensity is the sum of the baseline, trade-feedback, and news-pressure components, each using an exponential decay kernel, which keeps the conditional log-likelihood piecewise exponential and in principle estimable by maximum likelihood from trade timestamps and a timestamped news stream; because the trade-feedback and news loadings are only identified as products with their kernel amplitudes, the kernel amplitudes are normalized to one. The paper's central formal result is a diffusion theorem: under a clock law of large numbers and a finite conditional-moment bound on trade-level price impacts, the trade-time martingale component of log price converges, via a martingale functional central limit theorem, to a Brownian motion whose variance equals $m_J^2\bar\lambda$, i.e. $\sigma^2 = m_J^2\bar\lambda$, proportional to average trading intensity. The framework's implications are illustrated, not statistically tested, using one simulated 390-minute session generated by event-driven thinning of the fitted intensity, alongside three explicit empirical test designs (a variance-intensity regression, a VWAP curve fit, and a positive/negative-news symmetry test) that the paper proposes for future estimation on real transaction and news data but does not itself carry out.

## Results

- The paper is theoretical and illustrative rather than empirical: it reports one simulated 390-minute session rather than a statistical calibration to real market data.
- In the simulation, both positive and negative news events produce non-negative jumps in the news-pressure state, while the cumulative directional price component rises after positive news marks and falls after negative ones, illustrating the model's odd/even split.
- The paper proves that the trade-time component of the midpoint log price converges to a Brownian motion with variance $m_J^2\bar\lambda$, i.e. proportional to average trading intensity.
- It derives a closed-form VWAP execution profile in which, up to trade-size scaling, expected intraday volume rate is governed by the same three-component intensity (baseline, trade feedback, news pressure).
- It proposes three empirical tests it does not itself run: regressing realized variance on integrated intensity and news-event counts, comparing realized intraday volume shares to model-implied VWAP shares, and testing whether trading-intensity responses to positive and negative news of equal magnitude are statistically identical while price responses have opposite sign.
- The single illustrative simulation runs over a 390-minute regular session with a premarket burn-in, using equal opening and closing baseline amplitudes of 30.0, an opening-auction size of 400, and an exogenous news arrival rate of 0.03.

## Limitations

- The model is presented and illustrated with only a single simulated trading session; no real transaction or news data are fitted, so its empirical claims are proposed, untested predictions rather than results.
- Behavioral or asymmetric mappings from objective news into the signed mark (e.g. loss aversion) are explicitly left to future work; the model assumes squared-mark symmetry holds at the event level.
- Reader note: because trade sizes, news marks, and price impacts are all simulated under stylized parametric distributions chosen only to make the three intensity components visible, the reported simulation figures do not indicate real-world model fit or predictive accuracy.
- Reader note: the three proposed empirical tests (variance-intensity regression, VWAP curve fit, news-symmetry test) are specified but not executed anywhere in the paper.
