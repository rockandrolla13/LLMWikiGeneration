# Expert Opinion

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** January 10, 2017 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/expert-opinion.pdf)

## Summary

Marks calls 'expert opinion' an oxymoron and uses the polling failures of 2016 (Brexit, the Trump election, the Italian referendum) to show that expert forecasts are still only opinions. He criticises a partisan, certainty-projecting media, revisits the New York Post's football pickers, whose results cluster around 50% and add no value after the bookie's cost, and answers the macro questions he is asked most often (when will the Fed hike, what could go wrong, what inning are we in, what is the outlook for this country) by arguing they are unknowable or not useful. He notes that macro hedge fund indices earned returns close to zero, cites Buffett's test that information must be important and knowable, and closes with five favourite quotations on forecasting.

## Key Ideas

1. There are no facts about the future, only opinions; experts who state forecasts as certainties overstate their foresight.
2. 2016 polling failed in the UK, US and Italy; after the fact, opinions about the campaigns flip-flopped completely.
3. Modern media are partisan, rarely admit confusion, and act like 'portfolio managers who never mark to market'.
4. The New York Post's football experts clustered around 50%, gained little on 'best bets', and added virtually no value after fees.
5. Asking when the Fed will move is unhelpful: no one knows, and the expectation is priced in before the event.
6. Ask whether conditions are in an extended state (psychology, capital market openness) rather than 'what inning are we in'.
7. Macro hedge fund index returns over 1, 3 and 5 years to November 2016 may not differ statistically from zero.
8. Information worth pursuing must be important and knowable (Buffett); the macro is important but largely unknowable.

## Market Context

January 2017, nine weeks after Donald Trump's election. Brexit, the failed Italian referendum, India's cancellation of high-denomination rupee notes and upcoming French and German elections were in the news. The US stock market had its best week since 2014 right after the election, despite near-universal belief that a Trump win would hurt markets. Investors were preoccupied with Fed rate hikes since Bernanke's May 2013 taper remarks.

## Notable Claims

- FiveThirtyEight gave Clinton a 71.4% chance on election day.
- Trump won 304 to 227 in the Electoral College while losing the popular vote by about 2.9 million.
- Post experts were right 51.6% on average over 256 games.
- Oil fell 75% from June 2014 to February 2016 and no one predicted it.
- Average macro fund net return may be indistinguishable from zero.

## Quotes

> Of course there are no “facts” regarding most future events, just opinions.

> How would I know, and why do you care?

> Forecasts create the mirage that the future is knowable.

— Peter Bernstein, quoted by Marks
