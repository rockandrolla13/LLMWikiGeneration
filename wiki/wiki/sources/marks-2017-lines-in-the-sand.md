# Lines in the Sand

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** April 18, 2017 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/lines-in-the-sand.pdf)

## Summary

Marks examines subscription lines: bank loans to closed-end private funds, secured by LP capital commitments, that let funds invest or pay fees before calling LP capital. Because the lines cannot raise investments above committed capital, they defer capital calls rather than lever the fund, so they do not raise dollar profits or multiples of capital, and their interest is a permanent cost. They do raise reported IRR, soften the J-curve and lower the dollar preferred-return hurdle, which can bring incentive fees sooner and may tempt less disciplined GPs. He shows with examples that IRR, multiple of invested capital (MOC) and multiple of committed capital (MOCC) each miss part of the picture, so lines make IRR less meaningful and shift emphasis to multiples. He then raises risk scenarios: larger capital calls, LP strategic defaults, on-demand lines pulled in a crisis, and LPs over-committing uncalled capital, which could chain into forced selling and bank losses. Oaktree uses lines but began writing internal guidelines.

## Key Ideas

1. Subscription lines substitute for LP capital but cannot let a fund invest more than its commitments; they only defer capital calls.
2. Lines do not raise a fund's dollar profits, MOC or MOCC, and their interest costs permanently reduce lifetime gains.
3. Lines raise early and lifetime IRR, soften the J-curve, and shrink the dollar preferred-return hurdle for GP incentive fees.
4. No single metric (IRR, MOC, MOCC) captures commitment, invested amount, holding period and proceeds together.
5. As lines spread, IRR becomes suspect and evaluators must weight multiples of capital more heavily.
6. Fewer, larger capital calls raise the stakes of LP defaults, especially if LPs over-commit uncalled capital elsewhere.
7. On-demand lines pulled simultaneously in a crisis, or LP defaults cascading across funds, could create systemic risk for banks.
8. Financial security means surviving what can go wrong in bad times, not counting on things working on average.

## Market Context

April 2017. Subscription lines had become pervasive among closed-end funds in private equity, real estate, distressed debt and private credit, rates were low (lines cost below LIBOR+2%), and banks were demanding more information on LPs. Oaktree had begun using lines in some newer funds at clients' request.

## Notable Claims

- The most creditworthy LP commitments earn a 90% advance rate.
- A one-month doubling of a tiny investment can show an IRR of about 400,000%.
- A 3x MOCC is 20% IRR over six years but 11.6% over ten.
- Subscription line financing essentially just defers capital calls.
- Lines cast doubt on IRR.

## Quotes

> The bottom line is that essentially all subscription line financing does is defer the calling of LP capital.

> But I always hasten to point out that “should” isn’t the same as “will.”
