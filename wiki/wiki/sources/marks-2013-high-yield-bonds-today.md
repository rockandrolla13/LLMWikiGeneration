# High Yield Bonds Today

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** February 21, 2013 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2013-02-21-high-yield-bonds-today.pdf)

## Summary

A short client note, co-written with Sheldon Stone, answering whether high yield bonds were in a bubble after a strong 2012 and record-low yields. The authors argue that high prices reflect central banks holding down rates across all fixed income, not a high yield-specific excess. High yield is less exposed to rising rates than investment grade because higher coupons shorten duration, and rising rates usually come with a stronger economy and fewer defaults. Spreads of about 490 basis points sat toward the high end of Oaktree's historical range, enough to absorb a roughly 9% annual default rate before underperforming Treasurys. With a 5.7% yield, 1.4% default experience and half recovery, they project about 5% net a year before fees. They advise against market timing and favour risk control and selectivity over stretching for yield.

## Key Ideas

1. Low yields and high prices were common to all bonds and reflected central bank policy, not a bubble peculiar to high yield.
2. High yield bonds have shorter duration than investment grade bonds of the same maturity because of their larger coupons, so a given rate rise hurts them less.
3. Rising rates usually signal a growing economy, which improves creditworthiness and lowers defaults.
4. Look at spreads, not just yields: a ~490bp spread was toward the high end of the normal range and well above Oaktree's 1.4% average default loss experience.
5. Break-even default rate: the portfolio could suffer about 9% defaults a year and still match Treasurys, a level Oaktree had never seen in a single year.
6. Expected net return of about 5% = 5.7% yield minus 0.7% credit losses (1.4% defaults x roughly half loss).
7. In a low-upside market, superior returns come from minimizing mistakes rather than stretching for yield. Market timing in illiquid high yield is next to impossible and costly.

## Market Context

February 2013: after a strong 2012 for high yield, yields across fixed income were at historic lows because central banks worldwide were holding rates down. Corporate balance sheets had been strengthened by two years of refinancing, capital markets were awash in liquidity, the US economy showed some strength, and defaults were projected to stay below average for at least twelve months.

## Notable Claims

- Average spread on Oaktree's US high yield portfolios was about 490bp.
- Oaktree's default experience averaged 1.4% a year over 27 years.
- Portfolios could absorb about 9% annual defaults and still match Treasurys.
- 5.7% yield less 0.7% losses gives about 5% net.
- Market timing is impractical in high yield.

## Quotes

> In this type of environment, superior returns are more likely to be earned through minimizing mistakes than through stretching for yield.

> Rather than behaving aggressively, the search for return should involve risk control, caution, discipline and selectivity.

> On the other hand, they don’t have to equal last year’s return to warrant holding today.
