# Ditto

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** January 7, 2013 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2013-01-07-ditto.pdf)

## Summary

Marks explains that his memos return to the same themes because investors forget history and repeat mistakes. He defines a cycle as a chain reaction in which each event causes the next, using the real estate cycle as an example, then focuses on the cycle in attitudes toward risk, which he believes drives major market moves more than anything else. Because price is the main determinant of risk, investor behaviour creates most investment risk, and the riskiest thing is the belief that there is no risk. He lists what a successful contrarian needs, revisits his 2004 'Risk and Return Today' caution, and concludes that in 2013 investors are not thinking bullish but are acting bullish because near-zero rates force them to reach for yield, so caution is again warranted: 'ditto'.

## Key Ideas

1. Cycles in economics, investing and credit are chain reactions: each step is caused by the previous one, not merely followed by it.
2. Economies and profits have natural limits on their swings, but investor psychology has none, which is why prices swing more than profits.
3. Most investment risk comes from investor behaviour rather than from companies or securities, because participants set prices.
4. Asset quality does not determine risk; the Nifty Fifty lost about 90% while $1 in high yield at end-1979 grew to over $23.
5. The perversity of risk: the riskiest thing is the belief that there is no risk; high risk consciousness reduces risk.
6. A successful contrarian must see the herd's error, know intrinsic value, resist pressure, act opposite, and tolerate looking wrong for a while.
7. In 2013 investors were 'handcuff volunteers': not bullish in thought but forced by near-zero rates to buy risky credit.
8. Signs of a risk-prone market: record $812 billion leveraged-finance issuance, 6% average high yield, CCC issuance, dividend deals, CLO formation, rising LBO leverage.

## Market Context

January 2013, with central banks holding rates near zero. Investors were aware of the sluggish US recovery, fiscal imbalance and political dysfunction, problems in Europe, weak growth in Japan and a slowdown in China. Credit markets were booming: record leveraged-finance issuance, low yields, and buyout leverage near pre-crisis levels. Equities were still being disrespected.

## Notable Claims

- High yield index $1 at end-1979 grew to over $23.
- Record leveraged-finance issuance in 2012.
- Average high yield bond yields about 6%.
- LBO debt reached 5.5 times EBITDA in H2 2012.
- Fluctuating risk attitudes drive major market moves.

## Quotes

> The riskiest thing in the investment world is the belief that there’s no risk.

> The skeptic in times of moderation becomes a true believer at the top.

> These people aren’t buying because they want to, but because they feel they have to.
