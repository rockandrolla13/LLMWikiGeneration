# The Winds of Change

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** November 23, 2021 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/oaktreecaplibraries/memos/the-winds-of-change.pdf)

## Summary

With the near-term investment environment feeling like 'Groundhog Day' in late 2021, Marks turns to 'big macro' changes that will shape the coming years. Unlike the static backdrop of the 1950s-60s, when the Nifty Fifty were wrongly assumed to be undisruptable, today everything changes daily, so moats and 'defensive' labels matter less. Technology makes labour less necessary, raising worries about displaced workers, while Cathie Wood's deflation thesis prompts him to note that GDP can grow even as hours worked fall. He discusses shifting work patterns (gig work, WFH, the Great Resignation), the parlous state of US democracy (polarisation, gerrymandering, counter-majoritarian institutions, the filibuster), generational inequity driven by Baby Boomer spending and unfunded entitlements, the Fed's expanded roles and monetisation of debt, China's multiple simultaneous transitions, and the casual use of 'trillions'.

## Key Ideas

1. The Nifty Fifty showed that investors embrace the new while assuming nothing newer could displace it; at least half of those companies have since disappeared or been acquired.
2. In a fast-changing world, 'stable', 'defensive' and 'moat' are less relevant, and assumptions that tomorrow will look like yesterday need more scrutiny.
3. Information businesses scale with little capital or labour, so technology may boost GDP while adding to unemployment.
4. Pandemic-era changes in work (gig work, WFH, the Great Resignation, lower participation) may prove partly permanent.
5. US democracy is 'parlous': media profit from division, clustering and gerrymandering produce extremists, and Senate and Electoral College design allow minority rule; the filibuster trades tyranny of the majority for tyranny of the minority.
6. Generational inequity: Boomer political weight has driven deficit spending and avoidance of entitlement reform, leaving debt to future generations (national debt rose to 125% of GDP by 2021).
7. The Fed has taken on supporting markets (and possibly climate goals); its Treasury purchases funded much of government borrowing, crowding out price discovery.
8. China faces many simultaneous transitions; Marks' hunch is it will keep growing faster than the world, though not smoothly.

## Market Context

November 2021: economic resurgence since Q3 2020 continuing, markets rising since March 2020, inflation rising with no consensus on cause or persistence, the Delta variant, the Fed announcing a taper with rate hikes expected the next year, and the infrastructure bill signed on November 15, 2021.

## Notable Claims

- Labour force participation fell from 63.4% to 60.2% in 2020 and recovered only to 61.1%.
- About 7.4 million unemployed versus 11.2 million job openings.
- The 26 smallest states, with 17.7% of the population, could control the Senate.
- Washington approved over $9 trillion of Covid relief and infrastructure spending in 18 months without addressing Social Security or Medicare.
- Fed purchases funded 60-80% of government borrowing since 2010 (Larry Goodman).

## Quotes

> Today, unlike in the 1950s and ’60s, everything seems to change every day.

> Thus, if technology produces a big increase in output per hour worked, GDP can grow even if the number of hours worked declines.

> The ability to pass laws with a one-seat majority facilitates the tyranny of the majority. But the ability of 41 Senators to halt a bill’s progress permits the tyranny of the minority.
