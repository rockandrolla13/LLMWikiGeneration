# Risk and Return Today

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** October 27, 2004 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2004-10-27-risk-and-return-today.pdf)

## Summary

Marks explains why nearly every asset class in 2004 was priced to deliver low returns. He builds the capital market line from the riskless rate upward, with each riskier asset demanding a premium over the one before. In 2004 the line had both fallen, because the riskless rate was near 1% rather than about 4%, and flattened, because investors were fleeing low-yielding safe assets, had been rewarded by twenty years of risk-taking, and perceived little risk. He argues there was not more money in total, but that money moving en masse into small non-traditional asset classes swamped them. He concludes that 2004's mistakes would be errors of commission, that investors should not stretch for return, and that the answer is "special niches, special people."

## Key Ideas

1. Risky assets must appear to offer higher expected returns, but those returns cannot be dependable or the assets would not be risky.
2. The capital market line is a chain: each asset's required return is set relative to the safer asset before it, starting from the riskless rate.
3. In 2004 the line was lower because of near-1% short rates and flatter because risk aversion had fallen.
4. When investors are eager to escape safe assets, they accept less compensation to take on risk.
5. There was not more capital overall; capital shifting into small alternative asset classes overwhelmed them.
6. Investing mistakes alternate between omission and commission; in 2004 the likely mistake was buying too much and too aggressively.
7. The only fast way for prospective returns to rise is a price correction; the slow way is years of stagnation.
8. When opportunities are scarce, a manager should not try to repeat outsized past returns: take what the market gives.

## Market Context

Late 2004. The Fed had held short rates at multi-generational lows ("emergency rates") after the 2001-02 scandals and 9/11, though rates were inching up. Stock and bond returns were modest, default rates were at record lows, credit spreads were below average, and capital was flooding into private equity, hedge funds and distressed debt.

## Notable Claims

- Riskless rate near 1% in 2004 versus a typical 4%.
- Stressed bonds bought at 30-70% yields could be sold at 6-9%.
- Oaktree's newest distressed debt fund earned a 64% net IRR in year one.
- Low perceived risk means low prospective return.
- Default rates at record lows.

## Quotes

> if risky investments could be counted on to produce higher returns, they wouldn’t be risky.

> All we ever can do is take what they give us.

> There are times for aggressiveness. I think this is a time for caution.
