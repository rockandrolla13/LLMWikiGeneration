# Nobody Knows (Yet Again)

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** April 9, 2025 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/nobody-knows-yet-again.pdf)

## Summary

Written days after the April 2025 US tariff announcements, this memo reuses the title of Marks's post-Lehman 2008 memo and its March 2020 sequel. He recalls that in 2008 Oaktree invested without knowing the future, including $10 billion in Opportunities Fund VIIb, and argues that 'analyze the future' is an oxymoron. With all trade norms overthrown and no experts on modern large-scale trade wars, forecasts are even less reliable than usual. Yet waiting for certainty freezes investors, and not acting is itself an act. He grants that the administration's tariff goals are each desirable but stresses second- and third-order consequences: retaliation, higher prices, demand destruction, recession risk, shortages and a shift in the world order. He defends globalization and comparative advantage, warns that the US could lose its 'golden credit card' to fund deficits and $36 trillion of debt, and calls the tariffs an 'own goal' akin to Brexit. Costs will arrive quickly and any benefits only after years. In Oaktree's markets, wider spreads despite falling Treasury yields mean higher credit yields and more distress opportunities.

## Key Ideas

1. Investing during upheavals means acting without certainty; demanding confidence before acting leads to paralysis, and doing nothing is itself a decision.
2. Forecasts must come with a sense of their probability of being right, and in an unprecedented trade war that probability is lower than usual.
3. Tariff goals (manufacturing, exports, smaller deficits, onshoring, revenue) are each desirable, but second- and third-order consequences dominate in economics.
4. Tariffs are import taxes largely passed to consumers; reshoring is constrained by capacity, construction time, policy uncertainty and skilled labor.
5. Globalization and comparative advantage produced 80 years of broad prosperity; US post-war generosity was enlightened self-interest.
6. The US 'golden credit card' (persistent deficits funded by foreign demand for Treasurys) could be at risk if allies are antagonized.
7. Tariff costs arrive almost immediately while any benefits come after a multi-year adjustment; Marks calls the policy an 'own goal' like Brexit.
8. Wider spreads with lower Treasury yields lifted credit yields; expected distress means Oaktree may invest its latest opportunistic debt fund faster.

## Market Context

April 2025, a week after sweeping US 'reciprocal' tariffs larger than markets expected, with a sharp stock market decline. Before this the US economy was performing well and stocks were at all-time highs. Credit spreads widened while Treasury yields fell on a flight to safety, and US national debt stood at about $36 trillion.

## Notable Claims

- Oaktree had $10bn uninvested in Opportunities Fund VIIb in 2008.
- US consumer durable prices fell 40% in real terms 1995-2020.
- 2018 steel tariffs: 1,000 jobs saved vs 75,000 lost (reported, unverified by Marks).
- US fiscal deficits in each of the last 25 years.
- Volkswagen imports rose from 330 in 1950 to 400,000+ in 2012.

## Quotes

> In fact, I consider the phrase “analyze the future” one of the great oxymorons.

> But we also have to bear in mind that deciding not to act isn’t the opposite of acting; it’s an act in itself.

> I consider the tariff developments thus far to be what soccer fans call an “own goal”
