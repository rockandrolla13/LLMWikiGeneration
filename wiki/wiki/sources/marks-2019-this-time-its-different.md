# This Time It's Different

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** June 12, 2019 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/this-time-its-different.pdf)

## Summary

Marks reopens Anise Wallace's October 11, 1987 New York Times article on John Templeton's 'four most dangerous words', which appeared eight days before Black Monday. He argues that in 2019 the 'different' claims were about limits on future economic and market performance rather than valuation norms. He tests nine optimistic propositions: recessions can be avoided, permanent QE brings lasting prosperity, deficits are benign, national debt does not matter (the MMT view), growth can coexist with low inflation, rates stay lower for longer, an inverted yield curve is harmless, profitless companies can thrive, and growth beats value forever. For each he gives reasons for doubt. He notes that growth, low inflation, rising deficits, low rates and rising markets have rarely coexisted. He concludes that wide acceptance of such 'Goldilocks' beliefs signals third-stage bull market optimism and asset prices high relative to intrinsic value. He grants that technology may make 'different' right more often than Templeton's 20%.

## Key Ideas

1. Historical norms act like gravity on asset prices; bull markets reach extremes only when people decide those norms no longer apply.
2. In 2019 the 'different' claims targeted economic limits (recessions, deficits, debt, inflation, rates) rather than valuation multiples.
3. QE's effect may be partly psychological and diminishing, and suppressing small recessions may create moral hazard and a bigger one later.
4. MMT is an untested theory; its reliance on a country controlling its own currency hides the risk of a tipping point in US creditworthiness.
5. Inflation is 'mysterious', and the weak Phillips-curve link may not last.
6. Low price is not good value, but companies with better technology are not worth infinity, so value can return after winners are bid up to excess.
7. The nine propositions are all optimistic and mutually incompatible by historical standards; their popularity signals the third stage of a bull market.
8. Perception swings from 'flawless' to 'hopeless' while reality moves between 'pretty good' and 'not so hot'.

## Market Context

June 2019. The US recovery was in the twelfth month of its tenth year and about to become the longest on record. Unemployment was at a 50-year low with little inflation, and the yield curve had inverted. The Fed had paused hikes in January after the fourth-quarter 2018 selloff, and the 2017 tax cuts were widening deficits. MMT was being debated, and profitless tech companies such as Uber and Lyft were richly valued.

## Notable Claims

- Black Monday saw a 22% one-day fall, eight days after the Times article.
- The S&P 500 reached 32 times earnings at the start of the century.
- The US ran deficits in 46 of the last 50 years.
- The Fed raised rates nine times from late 2015 to 2¼-2½%.
- Australia's Future Fund holds A$154 billion.

## Quotes

> In investing, however, the truth usually lies somewhere between the extremes of infinite value and worthlessness.

> The word I use to describe inflation is “mysterious.”

> But it’s also essential that they not completely unlearn the lessons of the past.
