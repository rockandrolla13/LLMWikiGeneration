# Latest Update

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** March 19, 2020 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/weekly.pdf)

## Summary

A clients-only memo written in the first weeks of the Covid-19 crisis. Marks explains exponential growth and 'flattening the curve', expects strong containment measures that may conflict with civil liberties, and predicts unavoidable near-term pain: closures, job losses, supply-chain disruption and strained hospitals. He welcomes the speed of Fed and Treasury action but regrets that rates were cut from only 1.5% rather than 5%. On markets, he notes record daily declines followed by sharp rebounds, reads this as a sign that optimism has not yet been fully replaced by capitulation, and points out that gold, Bitcoin, risk-parity funds and a famous algorithmic fund all failed as safe havens. With high yield, loans and BB CLOs down 14-21%, he argues that no one can know the bottom, but bargains are materialising and it is a good time to invest some money.

## Key Ideas

1. Exponential growth is compounding: a constant growth rate produces ever-larger increases; flattening the curve means lowering that rate.
2. Containing the virus may require rules rather than suggestions, bringing public health into conflict with civil liberties.
3. Policy reacted far faster than in 2008, but the Fed had only 150 bp to cut versus about 500 bp in past easing cycles.
4. Big down days followed by big up days show optimism has not been eradicated; bottoms come when optimism is nowhere to be found.
5. There is no sure thing or silver bullet: gold fell, Bitcoin lost 47.6% in a month, and risk-parity funds lost double digits.
6. Forced selling for cash, redemptions and margin calls creates the emotional selling that produces the greatest bargains.
7. 'The bottom' is the day before the recovery begins, so it cannot be known; Oaktree buys when it can access value cheaply.

## Market Context

March 19, 2020, at the onset of the Covid-19 pandemic. The S&P 500 had several of its largest daily declines since 1940 (-7.6%, -9.5%, -12.0%, -5.2%) interspersed with large rebounds. The Fed had cut to zero with an emergency 100 bp cut on March 15 and relaunched the CPFF and PDCF; the ECB had just acted. Credit indices were down 14-21% from February 19 to March 18.

## Notable Claims

- Mnuchin warned unemployment could reach nearly 20%.
- Fed total cut only 150 bp versus ~500 bp historically.
- Bitcoin fell 47.6% in a month.
- High yield ex-energy yielded 8.9% at 787 bp after a 13.9% loss.
- The bottom cannot be known in advance.

## Quotes

> Typically, the bottom is reached only when optimism is nowhere to be found.

> “The bottom” is the day before the recovery begins.

> No one can argue that you should spend all your money today . . . but equally, no one can argue that you shouldn’t spend any.
