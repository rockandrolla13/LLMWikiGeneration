# Political Reality

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** August 17, 2016 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/political-reality.pdf)

## Summary

A follow-up to 'Economic Reality' (May 2016), written after the Brexit vote and during the US presidential campaign. Marks calls 'political reality' an oxymoron: economics is about choices and consequences, while candidates promise everything at no cost, and voters punish candour, as with Mondale's 1984 landslide loss. He reviews Brexit as political reality in action: a referendum called for party expediency, decided by 37% of registered voters on a simple majority, after false claims such as the £350 million weekly figure. He criticises Trump's economic claims on China, trade deficits, tariffs and debt renegotiation, citing Moody's Analytics projections of much larger deficits, while noting Clinton's own shortcomings. He explains the roots of discontent: globalization and automation benefit nations overall but hurt less-educated workers, and he expects populism and possible party realignment to persist. He proposes independent redistricting, top-two primaries, less money in politics, and avoiding referendums.

## Key Ideas

1. Economics is the study of choice and consequences; political campaigns promise 'all of the above' with no trade-offs.
2. Voters tend to reject candour about costs, as Mondale's 1984 defeat (13 electoral votes) illustrates.
3. The Brexit referendum was a 'dog's breakfast': called for expediency, decided by a simple majority, swayed by inaccurate claims, with no exit plan.
4. Trade deficits reflect better price/value from abroad; the US lost about ten times more jobs to productivity and automation than to China.
5. Protectionist tariffs raise consumer prices and invite retaliation; Moody's projected Trump's plan would sharply raise deficits and debt.
6. Globalization enriches nations but not all people; stagnant incomes for the less educated fuel populism, as in the 1930s.
7. Political realignment may produce a party of 'the Dissatisfieds', and a three-party system could send elections to the House.
8. Fixes: independent redistricting, top-two primaries, less money in politics, and avoiding referendums.

## Market Context

August 2016, weeks after the June 23 Brexit vote and the change of UK leadership to Theresa May, during the Trump-Clinton presidential campaign. Populist discontent over globalization, stagnant incomes and immigration was rising in the US and Europe. The memo is political-economic rather than about specific market moves.

## Notable Claims

- Brexit was decided by 37% of registered voters.
- PolitiFact rated 28% of Clinton's and 70% of Trump's checked statements Mostly False or worse.
- Moody's projected Trump's plan would raise the deficit to $3,151bn and debt to over $37tn by 2026.
- US manufacturing employment is down a third since 1979.
- Pennsylvania Democrats won 51% of 2012 House votes but only 5 of 18 seats.
