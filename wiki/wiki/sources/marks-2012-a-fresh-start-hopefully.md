# A Fresh Start (Hopefully)

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** November 19, 2012 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2012-11-09-a-fresh-start-hopefully.pdf)

## Summary

A rare openly political memo, written straight after the 2012 US presidential election, in which Marks tries to stay non-partisan. He reads the vote numbers as showing widespread disappointment with Obama, explains the demographic split between the parties, and notes that Hurricane Sandy may have swung the result. He criticises the electoral college for turning a small popular-vote edge into a large electoral margin. The investment-relevant core is fiscal: the US 'rhymes' with Greece, facing the fiscal cliff, trillion-dollar deficits and unaffordable entitlement promises. He argues gridlock is only acceptable in benign times, that real compromise means each side gives up things it promised, and that bipartisan action on the fiscal cliff is the clearest catalyst for better markets. He quotes Speaker Boehner's conciliatory speech and drafts his own imagined reply for Obama.

## Key Ideas

1. Obama won with 6.8 million fewer votes than in 2008, which Marks reads as disappointment among past supporters.
2. The two parties' constituencies differ sharply by demographics, and the Republican base is shrinking as a share of the electorate.
3. Many voter groups did not vote their pocketbooks, which runs against the usual assumption of economic self-interest.
4. The US is not Greece but it 'rhymes': excess spending, unfundable entitlements and rising debt require action.
5. Gridlock is tolerable only when conditions are benign; fiscal problems will not solve themselves.
6. Real compromise means both sides accept some of what the other wants, including things they swore to oppose.
7. Bipartisan resolution of the fiscal cliff was the clearest catalyst for markets at the time.

## Market Context

November 2012, just after Obama's re-election over Romney, with Republicans holding the House. The fiscal cliff of mandated tax rises and spending cuts loomed within six weeks. The Greek debt crisis was a reference point, and the US had suffered a credit downgrade in 2011 after the debt-ceiling battle.

## Notable Claims

- Obama's vote total fell by 6.8 million versus 2008.
- 41% of voters said Hurricane Sandy influenced their choice (CBS exit poll).
- The fiscal cliff could take 4% off GDP.
- Electoral vote margin 62%-38%.
- US debt downgrade followed the debt-ceiling fight.

## Quotes

> The bottom line is that if we don’t want to be Greece, we can’t act like Greece.

> No, compromise means, “We get some of what we want and you get some of what you want.”

> I find it shocking that the choice of a president for four years could turn on something as fickle as the weather.
