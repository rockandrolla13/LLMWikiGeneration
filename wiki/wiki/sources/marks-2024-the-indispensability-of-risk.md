# The Indispensability of Risk

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** April 17, 2024 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/the-indispensability-of-risk.pdf)

## Summary

A short memo that uses chess, backgammon and sports to argue that high returns cannot be earned without bearing risk. Prompted by a Wall Street Journal article by chess grandmaster Maurice Ashley on sacrifice, Marks separates 'sham' sacrifices with calculable payoffs (like buying a 10-year Treasury) from 'real' sacrifices whose gains are uncertain (most other investments). Because markets are reasonably efficient, investors must choose between no risk and little return, modest risk and modest return, or high uncertainty with a chance of big gains and permanent loss. He stresses the risk of taking too little risk, and says long-run success comes from many well-reasoned bets, some of which fail, not from avoiding all losers. The conclusion is that risk is necessary but not sufficient: it must be taken skillfully and with emotion under control.

## Key Ideas

1. Chess 'sham' sacrifices (calculable payoff) map to safe assets like Treasuries; 'real' sacrifices with uncertain gains map to most risky investments.
2. Investors face three choices: avoid risk for little return, take modest risk for modest return, or take high uncertainty for possible large gains and permanent loss.
3. Market efficiency, meaning other participants are not dummies, usually rules out big gains with little risk.
4. Outperformance requires accepting the possibility of underperformance, just as absolute gains require the possibility of loss.
5. Taking too little risk is itself a risk: individuals may not fund their living costs and professionals may miss benchmarks.
6. Long-run success, such as Buffett and Munger's, comes from many decent investments, a few big held winners, and relatively few big losers.
7. You should not expect to profit without risk, nor simply for taking risk; it must be taken skillfully.

## Market Context

Dated April 17, 2024. The memo is philosophical rather than market-driven; it was triggered by a Wall Street Journal article of April 12 by Maurice Ashley that Bruce Karsh sent to Marks. No specific market events are discussed.

## Notable Claims

- Marks frames three risk/return choices given uncertainty.
- Berkshire's record rests on three ingredients.
- A 10-year Treasury is a 'sham' sacrifice.
- Investment success depends on winner/loser ratio and relative magnitudes.

> You shouldn’t expect to make money without bearing risk, but you shouldn’t expect to make money just for taking risk.

— Magnus Carlsen, quoted by Marks
