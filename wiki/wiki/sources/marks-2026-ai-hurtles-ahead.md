# AI Hurtles Ahead

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** February 26, 2026 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/ai-hurtles-ahead.pdf)

## Summary

An addendum to Marks's December memo 'Is It a Bubble?', written after he asked Anthropic's Claude to prepare a tutorial on AI. He relays that a model is trained to reason rather than loaded with facts, then used through prompts at inference, and that prompt quality is a binding limit. He recounts Claude's rebuttal to the 'pattern matching is not thought' critique and its argument that the economic question is whether AI does the work. He stresses AI's unprecedented adoption speed and its jump from chat (Level 1) to tool use (Level 2) to autonomous agents (Level 3), quoting Matt Shumer's account of the February 5 model releases. He lists limits: weakness on novel situations, hallucination, context-window limits, and excess credibility. For investing, AI should beat humans at processing available quantitative data, raising the bar for active managers, but superior humans may keep an edge on novel situations, qualitative judgment and risk intuition. On bubbles, he calls the technology real and likely underestimated but says that does not make AI assets fairly priced, flags circular revenue, and advises a moderate, selective position. A postscript worries about job losses.

## Key Ideas

1. Training teaches a model how to reason; inference is driven by user prompts, so weak prompting causes AI's potential to be underestimated.
2. AI can be seen as forming hypotheses about the future from past patterns, which limits it in genuinely novel situations.
3. AI's adoption speed (about 400 million users and 75-80% of companies within two years) far outpaces past technologies such as the computer.
4. The move from Level 2 tool-using AI to Level 3 autonomous agents shifts AI from productivity tool to labour substitute.
5. Readily available quantitative information cannot produce superior returns, and AI will process it better than people, raising the bar for active managers.
6. Human edge may persist in judging implications, assessing qualitative factors, dealing with novelty, and sensing risk with skin in the game.
7. AI is real and probably underestimated, but infrastructure build-outs historically cause malinvestment, and some AI revenue is circular.
8. Neither go all-in (risk of ruin) nor stay all-out (risk of missing out); take a moderate, selective position.

## Market Context

Late February 2026, about three months after 'Is It a Bubble?' (December 9). OpenAI's GPT-5.3 Codex and Anthropic's Opus 4.6 had been released on February 5, software stocks had sold off from February 3 on AI-disruption fears, AI inference capacity was said to be supply-constrained, and OpenAI and Anthropic had not yet listed publicly.

## Notable Claims

- Generative AI is used by about 400 million people and 75-80% of companies.
- Many software stocks fell about 7% on February 3, starting a rout.
- More money now goes into inference capex than training capex.
- Waymo handles about one-fifth of San Francisco taxi trips.
- Claude's tutorial placed AI at Level 1 in 2023, Level 2 in 2024 and Level 3 now.

## Quotes

> But the economic question isn’t “does AI truly understand?” The economic question is “does AI do the work?”

— Anthropic's Claude, from the AI tutorial Marks quotes

> Since AI seems amorphous and little understood, I think its potential is more likely to be underestimated today than exaggerated.

> A moderate position, applied with selectivity and prudence, seems like the best approach.
