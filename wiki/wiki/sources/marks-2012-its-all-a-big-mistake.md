# It’s All a Big Mistake

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** June 20, 2012 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2012-06-20-its-all-a-big-mistake.pdf)

## Summary

Marks argues that superior investing is entirely about mistakes: one side of most trades is wrong, and active management is the search for the other side's errors. He contrasts the efficient market hypothesis with real investors who are not consistently objective or rational, and lists the behavioral sources of mispricing (bias, capital rigidity, psychological excess, herd behavior). He uses Oaktree's distressed debt record under Bruce Karsh as a case study of an 'anti-error business model' that profits from flawed underwriting in good times and panic selling in bad times. He warns against acting on all forecasts with equal confidence, and closes with JP Morgan's 2012 hedging loss to show the many ways a hedge can fail. Risk control, he concludes, is a mindset that weights avoiding mistakes as heavily as doing great things.

## Key Ideas

1. For one side of a transaction to be a big success, the other side usually has to have been a big mistake; win/lose trades outnumber win/win.
2. Markets may be quick to incorporate information but not sure to incorporate it correctly; 'inefficiencies' is the academic word for mistakes.
3. Active management makes sense only if pricing mistakes exist in a market and the manager can identify and exploit them.
4. Mispricings persist because of bias, capital rigidity (forced buyers or sellers), psychological excesses and herd behavior.
5. Distressed debt is an anti-error model: it profits from overly optimistic underwriting ('stacking wood for the bonfire') and from panicked sellers.
6. Pro-cyclical behavior and trend extrapolation are among the biggest destroyers of capital, since most things regress to the mean.
7. It is one thing to have an opinion and another to act as if it is right; acting on all forecasts with equal confidence is a major error.
8. Hedging can fail through basis risk, the wrong hedge ratio, timing mismatch and illiquidity, as JP Morgan's loss showed.

## Market Context

Written in June 2012, during the European sovereign debt crisis (Marks mentions uncertainty about Greece staying in the EU) and shortly after JP Morgan disclosed multi-billion-dollar losses on a credit hedge, which politicians used to argue for tighter bank regulation.

## Notable Claims

- Oaktree distressed debt track record.
- All Oaktree distressed funds had been profitable.
- The 2005-07 build-up produced the best distressed opportunities Marks had seen.
- Forced selling by CLOs in 2008 created mispricings.
- Investors in 1978 refused B-rated bonds at any price.

## Quotes

> Active management has to be seen as the search for mistakes.

> “it’s one thing to have an opinion, and something very different to act as if it’s right.”

— one of Oaktree's favourite sayings, quoted by Marks

> Forecasting error is much less likely to prove fatal in the absence of excess conviction.
