# Will It Be Different This Time?

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** November 25, 1996 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1996-11-25-will-it-be-different-this-time.pdf)

## Summary

Marks sets a 1996 Wall Street Journal story claiming the business cycle has been tamed beside a New York Times article from early October 1987 that made the same claim days before the crash. He lists the current reasons offered for why high stock valuations are safe, and he answers that cyclicality is one of the few constants because cycles come from human behavior and short financial memory. He points to late-cycle warning signs: declining skepticism, aggressive credit, high acquisition prices and narrow market breadth. He also describes capitulation, where long-holdout investors give in and buy at the top. He admits his caution may come from his own early career in the 1973-74 bear market. He says Oaktree stays fully invested with defensive overlays rather than acting on a market-timing call.

## Key Ideas

1. Every era produces a fresh list of reasons why the old cycle will not recur, and the list grows longer as prices rise.
2. Cycles come from herd behavior and psychological excess, so they are unlikely to disappear with economic change.
3. Capitulation happens at tops too: holdouts who finally buy extend the trend briefly but mark the late stage of the cycle and leave no buyers to power a further rise.
4. Late-cycle signals include falling skepticism and discipline, aggressive credit extension, high acquisition prices, too much money chasing too few deals, and narrow breadth.
5. The right time to argue that things will be better is when the market is depressed and others sell at giveaway prices, not at record highs.
6. Holding an opinion is different from acting as if it is right: Oaktree takes defensive steps but stays fully invested.
7. The 1988 distressed debt fund faced investors who said there would be no recession, and the 1990 recession then gave it plenty to buy.

## Market Context

Late 1996 bull market: the expansion was 67 months old, the Dow hit a record 6547 on the day Marks wrote, and investors pointed to 401(k) inflows, buybacks, technology and the Internet, and deficit discipline to justify high valuations. Consumer debt, defaults and bankruptcies were high, and market breadth was narrow.

## Notable Claims

- The October 1987 crash followed an optimistic Times article within days.
- Economists' consensus was for continued growth despite the expansion's age.
- The 1996 expansion had already outlasted the postwar average.
- Oaktree's 1988 distressed debt fund returned 29% gross per year.
- Nifty-fifty stocks fell 70-90% in the early 1970s.

## Quotes

> And I feel cyclicality is one of the few constants in the economy and markets

> We believe strongly that "it's one thing to have an opinion but quite another thing to act as if it's right."

> So we conclude that most of the time, the future will look a lot like the past, with both up cycles and down cycles.
