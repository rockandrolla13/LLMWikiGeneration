# What's Going On?

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** May 6, 2003 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2003-05-16-whats-going-on.pdf)

## Summary

Marks describes a paradigm shift in return expectations after the 2000-02 bear market: institutions that once hit 8-10% targets by loading up on equities now expect only mid-single-digit stock returns and low bond yields, so the question has become how much can be earned safely. He argues that two decades of outsized equity gains borrowed from the future, then turns to the most violent cycle he had seen, in distressed and high yield debt. In mid-2002, after Enron, WorldCom and other scandals, Oaktree invested over $2 billion in distressed bonds at death's-door prices; within six months the market swung to panic buying, Tyco bonds bought at a 24% yield traded at about 4%, and high yield spreads fell from over 1,000bp to about 600bp. He attributes this to capital flows into small niche markets and closes with a cat-in-a-tree metaphor: the carrot of high recent returns and the stick of low safe returns push investors out on riskier limbs, so they may have to lower target returns.

## Key Ideas

1. The old 8-10% institutional target was easy when equities 'normally' returned 9-11%; expectations for stocks fell to roughly 4-8%, so the target itself is in question.
2. Above-average equity gains of the 1980s-90s borrowed from the future; high P/Es imply single-digit returns ahead.
3. Orphaned small and mid-cap stocks losing analyst coverage may make parts of the equity market less efficient.
4. The 2002-03 distressed debt cycle was the fastest and most extreme Marks had seen: forced selling after corporate scandals, then a six-month reversal into panic buying.
5. In the short run capital flows dominate; dollars leaving the huge equity market swamp small niche markets like high yield and distressed debt.
6. Everything else equal, a higher return to date means a lower return in the future.
7. Cat-tree-carrot-stick metaphor: high recent returns lure and low safe yields push investors into riskier strategies; risk never looks like risk when it is generating a high return.
8. Oaktree response: candour about conditions, limiting assets under management, and sticking to value investing.

## Market Context

Spring 2003, after the 2000-02 equity bear market and the 2002 wave of corporate scandals (Enron, Tyco, Adelphia, Global Crossing, Arthur Andersen, WorldCom). Interest rates at 40-year lows after Fed easing, equities out of favour, and a sharp rally in distressed debt and high yield bonds from late 2002 driven by capital inflows.

## Notable Claims

- Oaktree invested more than $2 billion in distressed debt in summer 2002.
- Distressed positions bought in 2002 gained more than 55% gross (41% net) from November to April.
- Tyco bonds went from a 24% yield in May 2002 to about 4% by January 2003.
- High yield spreads went from over 1,000bp to about 600bp.
- S&P 500 averaged over 17% from 1978 through 1999 with no year down more than 5%.

## Quotes

> Risk never looks like risk when it's generating a high return.

> Thus, everything else being equal, a higher return to date means a lower return in the future.

> in the short run it's capital flows that have the most profound impact of all.
