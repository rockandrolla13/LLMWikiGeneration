# The Folly of Certainty

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** July 17, 2024 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/oaktreecaplibraries/memos/the-folly-of-certainty.pdf)

## Summary

A short memo prompted by a New York Times report that Biden's campaign chair had 'no doubt' he would win. Marks argues that no one can be certain about outcomes shaped by psychology, irrationality and randomness, and illustrates this with politics (the 2016 election and the market rally that followed), macroeconomics (the Fed's 'transitory' inflation call, the recession that did not arrive, and repeatedly delayed rate cuts) and markets, which swing far more than GDP or profits because of investor emotion. He cites Galbraith on the brevity of financial memory and the 'specious association of money and intelligence', revisits his 2020 memos on intellectual humility, and closes with Krejcikova's 125-to-1 Wimbledon win and the attempt on Trump's life as reminders that prediction is largely a loser's game.

## Key Ideas

1. Macro forecasters cannot incorporate all variables plus randomness, so avoid words like 'will', 'won't', 'always' and 'never'.
2. A forecast that came true does not show the unexpected was unlikely; the chance of error was present either way.
3. The 2021-24 Fed cycle shows serial consensus errors: 'transitory' inflation, an expected recession that did not come, and rate cuts priced in but not delivered.
4. Being right on the macro can still lose money: those who correctly expected no cuts from October 2022 missed a roughly 50% S&P 500 gain.
5. Markets are far more volatile than the economy or profits because of investor emotion, so homo economicus assumptions fail.
6. Galbraith's 'specious association of money and intelligence': success in investing does not confer expertise elsewhere.
7. Intellectual humility ('I might be wrong') keeps investors out of trouble; certainty that is wrong can be catastrophic.

## Market Context

July 2024, weeks after the June 27 Biden-Trump debate and just after the attempt on Donald Trump's life. The Fed had not yet cut rates in 2024 despite market expectations, the consensus pointed to a first cut in September, and the stock market kept hitting new highs.

## Notable Claims

- Stock market rose over 30% in the 14 months after Trump's 2016 win.
- Market priced six cuts when the dot plot showed three.
- Correct no-cut forecasters missed a ~50% S&P 500 gain.
- 40-year standard deviations: GDP 1.8%, profits 9.4%, S&P 500 price 13.1%.
- Economist recession consensus arrives when recession is over.
