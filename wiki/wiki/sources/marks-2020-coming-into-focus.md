# Coming into Focus

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** October 13, 2020 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/coming-into-focus57a8e688-a1d0-4b59-928e-9e6ff1f8b5a6.pdf)

## Summary

A follow-up to Time for Thinking written in October 2020. Marks argues the Covid downturn is not a normal cycle: an exogenous, non-economic shock caused the recession, so economic stimulus alone cannot cure it and recovery depends on controlling the disease and on continued fiscal support, which partisan gridlock was blocking. He sets out seven ways ultra-low interest rates lift asset prices, from stimulus and higher discounted values to a lower capital market line, reaching for return and reopened credit markets. He weighs arguments that the FAAMG tech leaders are stronger than the Nifty Fifty while warning that 'no price too high' marks all bubbles. He contrasts 2020 with 1990-91, 2001-02 and 2008-09, notes that the Fed and Treasury removed the usual bargains, discusses moral hazard, inflation risk and inequality, and concludes that in a low-return world he leans toward defense.

## Key Ideas

1. The Covid downturn was caused by an exogenous health shock, so it needs disease control plus fiscal support, not just economic stimulus.
2. Government payments in 2020 were support to replace lost income, not stimulus, and the need for them had not ended.
3. Low rates raise asset prices in seven ways: stimulus, higher DCF values, a lower capital market line, higher P/E multiples, Fed bond buying, reaching for return, and reopened capital markets.
4. When the risk-free rate falls, all assets can be fairly priced relative to each other while all prospective returns sit near historic lows.
5. Tech leaders may be stronger than the Nifty Fifty, but 'no price too high' is the hallmark of all bubbles, and index averages mask two very different groups of stocks.
6. Unlike past crises, the Fed and Treasury gave everyone money and nerve, so the usual bargains for distressed investors did not appear.
7. Rescues create moral hazard and a perceived 'Powell put'; inflation, stagflation or deflation are all possible, and Oaktree will not bet on macro forecasts.
8. In a low-return world every strategic option has a downside, and Marks leans toward defense.

## Market Context

October 2020, seven months into the Covid-19 pandemic and less than a month before the US presidential election. The Fed had cut rates to 0-0.25% in March and bought over $2.3 trillion of securities by mid-July; the S&P 500 had recovered to near its February high; high yield issuance was at a record; a second fiscal package was stalled by partisan conflict; Europe was seeing new case spikes and vaccines were still some time off.

## Notable Claims

- Speed of the 2020 equity collapse.
- Scale of 2020 Fed bond buying versus the GFC.
- Record high yield issuance in 2020.
- Arithmetic of lower rates on P/E ratios.
- Mortgage delinquency in mid-2020.

## Quotes

> Those last four words are, in my opinion, the essential component in – and the hallmark of – all bubbles.

> Other investors’ lack of money and nerve in past crises made them great times for buying. Today, thanks to the Fed and Treasury, everyone’s got a lot of both. That makes things much tougher.

> I put it simply: just because something has potential negative consequences doesn’t mean you shouldn’t do it.
