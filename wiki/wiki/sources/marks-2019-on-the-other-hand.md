# On the Other Hand

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** July 26, 2019 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/on-the-other-hand.pdf)

## Summary

Prompted by a Ruffer question about whether the Fed can keep market dislocations at bay, this memo examines Fed interest-rate management in mid-2019. Marks contrasts the first-level chain investors assume (rate cuts lead to stimulus, profits and higher stocks) with second-level questions about why the Fed is cutting. He recalls that the 50 bp cut of September 2007 was greeted warmly, yet the S&P 500 later bottomed more than 50% lower. He lists how low rates help (cheaper borrowing, higher asset values through lower discount rates, more risk appetite) and how they hurt (inflation risk, penalising savers, reaching for yield, bubbles, financial instability, less room to cut later). He stresses that rate cuts partly work through belief in them, and that the Fed faces risks of acting too much, too little, too soon or too late. He ends by asking whether postponing recessions just makes the eventual one worse and revives moral hazard.

## Key Ideas

1. Investors treat rate cuts as a simple buy signal; second-level thinkers ask what weakness the Fed is responding to.
2. A larger-than-usual rate cut can be read as bad news, like a doctor reaching for a huge needle.
3. Falling interest rates raise asset prices through lower discount rates, which Marks sees as the dominant financial feature of the 2010s.
4. Low rates push savers to reach for yield, encourage leverage and can create bubbles and financial instability.
5. Low rates leave central banks with less room to use their main tool in the next downturn.
6. Monetary policy works partly through psychology: belief that rate cuts will work helps make them work.
7. Recessions may be postponable rather than avoidable, and repeatedly warding off small ones may produce a bigger one later.

## Market Context

July 2019. The Fed had abandoned planned rate hikes early in the year and markets expected cuts. The S&P 500 crossed 3,000 for the first time, unemployment was at a 50-year low, the expansion had become the longest on record, a US-China trade war was the main uncertainty, and President Trump was publicly pressuring Powell for lower rates.

## Notable Claims

- Fed cut 50 bp on September 18, 2007.
- S&P 500 bottomed more than 50% below its level on the first cut day.
- S&P 500 up about 20% in 2019 on the Fed pivot.
- Low rates drive asset prices via discounting.
- Economy was healthy in mid-2019.

## Quotes

> If you went to the doctor for an ailment and he pulled out a huge hypodermic needle, would you take that as good news or bad?

> In other words, there’s the risk of being unresponsive or over-responsive, along with the risk of doing too much or too little, and of doing it too soon or too late.

> Clearly, prevailing opinion regarding Fed management matters a great deal in the efficacy of its actions.
