# It's Not Easy

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** September 9, 2015 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2015-09-09-its-not-easy.pdf)

## Summary

Built around Charlie Munger's remark that investing is not supposed to be easy and anyone who finds it easy is stupid, this memo explains why markets eliminate easy profits and why superior results need second-level thinking. Marks restates the second-level thinking chapter of The Most Important Thing, using Keynes's beauty contest and Graham's voting and weighing machines. He argues that what everyone likes is usually overpriced, and introduces 'the perversity of risk': risk is highest where it is least perceived, and investors confuse fundamental risk with investment risk, which depends on price relative to value. He then tests sixteen pieces of conventional wisdom (efficient markets, risky assets deliver high returns, liquidity means safety and others) and shows each is only partly true. He closes with lessons from the August 2015 China-driven sell-off and the point that alpha is the only tool that is not two-edged.

## Key Ideas

1. Market participants' collective effort removes easy opportunities, so superior returns need an edge others lack.
2. Second-level thinking asks what the consensus expects and how the price reflects it, not just whether an asset is good.
3. What everyone likes has usually been bid up; big returns come from buying what everyone underestimates.
4. The perversity of risk: risk is highest when people believe there is none, and low when everyone thinks something is too risky.
5. Investment risk is set by price relative to intrinsic value, not by asset quality; investors must separate fundamental risk from investment risk.
6. Many investing bromides (risky assets yield more, liquidity means safety, profits prove good decisions) are only sometimes true.
7. In a downdraft, the market is a sentiment barometer, not an analyst; selling to feel comfortable ignores price versus value.
8. Every tactic is two-edged except alpha, and the average investor cannot beat the market after fees.

## Market Context

September 2015, right after a sharp global sell-off triggered by slowing Chinese growth, yuan depreciation and a Chinese market correction of nearly 40%. The S&P 500 fell 11% in eight days, a BNY Mellon pricing glitch hit 1,200 funds, and commentators worried about reduced liquidity under the Volcker rule.

## Notable Claims

- Munger's quote.
- S&P 500 fell 11% in August 2015 and Chinese equities nearly 40%.
- BNY Mellon's SunGard glitch left 1,200 funds unpriced.
- Nifty Fifty traded at 80-90 times earnings.
- Average investor underperforms after fees.

## Quotes

> the riskiest thing in the world is the widespread belief that there’s no risk

> But something about which I feel strongly is that it’s not the things you buy and sell that make you money; it’s the things you hold.

> There’s only one thing in the investment world that isn’t two-edged, and that’s “alpha”: superior insight or skill.
