# Bull Market Rhymes

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** May 26, 2022 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/bull-market-rhymes.pdf)

## Summary

Using the adage that history does not repeat but rhymes, Marks examines the recurring behavioural themes of the 2020-21 bull market and its 2022 correction, stating he is not forecasting direction. He argues that cycles arise from psychology-driven excesses and corrections, defines a bull market by its psychology rather than a 20% threshold, and notes that the 2020 rally skipped the usual first stage of the three stages of a bull market. Recurring features included a 'new, new thing' that history cannot discount (FAAMG super stocks, unprofitable IPOs, SPACs, Robinhood and meme stocks, crypto), Galbraith's brevity of financial memory, a race to the bottom in lending standards, and rising leverage. The leaders of the boom fell hardest, and price co-movement reflects psychology rather than fundamentals.

## Key Ideas

1. Cycles exist because psychology drives excesses to the upside, corrections, excesses to the downside and recoveries; the S&P 500 is rarely near its ~10% average in any year.
2. A bull market is best defined by its psychology and behaviour, not by a 20% price rule.
3. The 2020 rally was unusual: driven at first by Fed rate cuts and stimulus that forced 'handcuff volunteers' into risk, it largely skipped the first two stages of a bull market.
4. Bull markets need a rationale based on something new that history cannot be used to discount; investors are not forgetful, the dream of getting rich simply outweighs prudence.
5. Super stocks (FAAMGs), unprofitable IPOs, SPACs, commission-free retail trading and crypto illustrated the 'new thing' and 'this time it's different' themes.
6. The race to the bottom: in good times lenders accept lower rates and fewer covenants, so the worst loans are made in the best of times.
7. 'What the wise man does in the beginning, the fool does in the end'; bull market psychology should worry, not encourage, investors.
8. Prices reflect fundamentals and how people view them; views change faster than fundamentals, and the biggest winners of a boom often suffer the biggest declines.

## Market Context

May 2022. After the pandemic crash of February-March 2020, Fed rate cuts and fiscal stimulus fuelled a 2020-21 boom in tech, SPACs, meme stocks and crypto. By mid-2022 inflation and Russia's invasion of Ukraine had triggered a correction: the S&P 500 approached a 20% decline on May 20, the Nasdaq was down 27.4% for the year, and Bitcoin was off more than half from its 2021 high.

## Notable Claims

- S&P 500 fell by one-third in 34 days from February to March 2020.
- SPAC formation soared from 10 in 2013 to 613 in 2021.
- The average de-SPAC'd company since 2020 trades at $5.25 versus a $10 issue price.
- Robinhood fell 88% from its post-IPO high in under a year.
- 22% of Nasdaq stocks were down at least 50% as of May 20, 2022.

## Quotes

> The events of investment history don’t repeat, but familiar themes do recur, especially behavioral themes.

> This is why it’s often said that “the worst of loans are made in the best of times.”

> It’s hard to imagine a full-throated bull market arising in the absence of something that’s never been seen or heard before.
