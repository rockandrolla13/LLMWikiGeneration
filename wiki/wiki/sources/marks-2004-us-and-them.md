# Us and Them

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** May 7, 2004 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2004-05-07-us-and-them.pdf)

## Summary

Marks extends his earlier split between the "I know" and "I don't know" schools into a full syndrome of correlated investor traits. The "us" investor is defensive, risk-obsessed, contrarian, value-minded, patient and absolute-return oriented. This investor averages down, takes profits, is comfortable with cash, limits assets under management and thinks the market is often wrong. The "them" investor is confident and optimistic, follows trends and focuses on growth and momentum. This investor worries about underperforming, dislikes holding cash and trusts market efficiency. Marks applies the split to 2004: optimists cite recovery and low rates, while he worries that valuations depend on rates staying at record lows. He uses an unnamed investor, revealed as Warren Buffett, as the model "us" investor. He ends by noting that contrarians need others' mistakes to profit from.

## Key Ideas

1. Investor traits cluster: views on foreknowledge go together with attitudes to risk, style, cash and market efficiency.
2. Few people see both luck and skill; most see only one column of Taleb's luck-versus-skill table.
3. Defensive investors worry about losers bought, while aggressive investors worry about winners missed.
4. Low interest rates prop up valuations; rates have little room to fall and much room to rise, so p/e ratios have more room down than up.
5. Record-low rates pushed investors toward riskier assets: 6-8% high yield, 9-11% market-neutral hedge funds, 15-20% private equity.
6. Too much capital hurts returns, so the cautious investor limits assets under management.
7. Contrarian opportunities exist only because other investors make mistakes.

## Market Context

May 2004, after a strong 2003 rebound. The market had moved sideways for a few months, money market yields were around 1%, and rates were at record lows. Concerns included trade and budget deficits, US reliance on foreign capital, the dollar, high consumer debt and terrorism. Capital was moving into hedge funds.

## Notable Claims

- Low money-market and Treasury yields made riskier assets look attractive.
- Low rates justified p/e ratios in the high 20s.
- Marks's bank loan rate hit 22¼% in 1980.
- Buffett's cash holdings tripled in 2003.
- Distressed bond yields collapsed from 2002 to 2004.

## Quotes

> Ours obsesses about the losers he might buy or hold, while the other dwells on the opportunities he might miss.

> In order for us to be contrarians, there has to be someone to be contrary to.

> The best opportunities for investment returns aren’t created by companies, exchanges or paper securities; they result from the mistakes other investors make.
