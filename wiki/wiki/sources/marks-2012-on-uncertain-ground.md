# On Uncertain Ground

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** September 11, 2012 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2012-09-11-on-uncertain-ground.pdf)

## Summary

Marks says the world seems more uncertain than at any time in his life and surveys why: sluggish post-2008 growth as consumers and governments delever, low confidence and weak business investment, the unresolved euro-zone crisis, U.S. fiscal problems (entitlements, the fiscal cliff, $1-3 trillion of underfunded state pensions), political gridlock, central-bank-engineered low returns, inequality, geopolitical hotspots, and a possible hard landing in China. Against this he weighs three positives: things may not be as bad as feared, many asset prices (equities, high yield spreads, real estate) are reasonable, and investor psychology is subdued, which he sees as the biggest plus because the riskiest condition is a belief that there is no risk. He rejects 'risk-on, risk-off' macro trading and concludes that the right stance is to balance risk aversion with pursuit of return in well-priced corporate securities: 'Move forward, but with caution.'

## Key Ideas

1. Economic growth is not automatic; it depends on population, infrastructure, aspiration, productivity and benign exogenous events, all less favourable in the U.S. now.
2. Decades of expanding credit boosted growth but raised precariousness; simultaneous delevering is contractionary.
3. It is as wrong to see things as hopeless as to consider an environment risk-free.
4. The riskiest thing is widespread belief that there's no risk; risk is low when investors behave prudently and high when they don't.
5. Low central-bank rates force 'handcuff volunteers' out on the risk curve even when they see the risks.
6. Short-term macro forecasting and 'risk-on, risk-off' trading could add value if done right but can't be done consistently.
7. Three strategy questions: expect prosperity or not; fear losing money or missing opportunity; be aggressive or cautious.
8. Hickman's 1900-1943 study shows lower-rated corporate bonds returned more, supporting corporate credit investing in uncertain times.

## Market Context

September 2012: slow U.S. recovery with businesses hoarding cash, the approaching 'fiscal cliff' of automatic tax rises and spending cuts in January 2013, a Romney-Obama election with Paul Ryan as VP nominee, the ECB's newly announced bond-buying programme amid the PIIGS crisis, municipal bankruptcies, a slowing China, near-zero T-bill rates and a 10-year Treasury near 1.7%.

## Notable Claims

- Interest on federal debt consumes roughly 10% of federal taxes even at ultra-low rates.
- U.S. national debt of $16tn is dwarfed by $50-90tn of unfunded future benefits.
- Underfunded pension plans of $1-3tn are a ticking time bomb.
- The S&P 500 traded at more than 30x earnings in 1999; the P/E has since more than halved.
- Hickman found lower-quality bonds returned more over 1900-1943.

## Quotes

> It's essential, however, to remember that it can be just as wrong to see things as hopeless as it is to consider an environment risk-free.

> In short, risk is low when investors behave prudently and high when they don't.

> In the investing world, even doing nothing is doing something.
