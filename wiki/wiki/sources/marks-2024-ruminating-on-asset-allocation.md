# Ruminating on Asset Allocation

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** October 22, 2024 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/default-document-library/ruminating-on-asset-allocation.pdf)

## Summary

Prompted by client meetings in Australia, Marks offers a unified theory of asset allocation. He argues that at bottom there are only two asset classes, ownership and debt, which differ in kind: owners get an uncertain residual with upside and downside, while lenders get a contractual, 'fixed outcome' return. The single most important portfolio decision is the targeted risk posture -- the balance between offense (growth) and defense (capital preservation) -- and all other allocation choices are implementation details. Using notional probability distributions, he shows ownership has a higher expected return with a wider spread, debt a lower but tighter one, and blends sit in between, which he presents as another view of his 2006 risk/return chart with distributions superimposed. In an efficient market no point on the continuum is better and leverage versus riskier assets is a wash, but markets are not efficient in the sense of always being right, so skilled selection and alpha managers can help. He concludes that with non-investment-grade credit yielding about 7% public and 10% private, many investors should build a programme to raise credit allocations.

## Key Ideas

1. There are only two fundamental asset classes: ownership (equity, real estate, private equity, real assets) and debt (bonds, loans, MBS); the difference is one of kind, not degree.
2. Debt can be thought of as 'fixed outcome' investing because the return is known in advance if the borrower pays.
3. The most important decision is the targeted risk posture (offense vs defense); you cannot emphasise both capital preservation and maximum growth.
4. The absolute level of portfolio risk must be consciously targeted, not left as a by-product of seeking good risk-adjusted returns.
5. Riskier positions bring higher expected return, a wider range of outcomes and worse bad outcomes -- Marks's distributions-on-the-risk/return-line chart.
6. Asset allocation models depend on historical return, risk and correlation inputs, so they risk garbage in, garbage out.
7. In an efficient market leverage and riskier assets are equivalent and there is no alpha; real markets can be efficient at absorbing information yet wrong in price, so skill can add value.
8. With credit yields of roughly 7% public and 10% private, competitive with historical equity returns and more dependable, investors should raise credit allocations in steps.

## Market Context

October 2024: about two years into what Marks calls a 'sea change' to higher interest rates after the 2009-21 low-rate era; credit yields around 7% public and 10% private; Marks recently back from client visits in Australia. Higher credit returns had been available a year or two earlier.

## Notable Claims

- Non-investment-grade credit offers roughly 7% on public and 10% on private credit.
- Marks moved from Citibank's equity research to its bond department in 1978 and discovered yield-to-maturity calculation.
- The term 'asset allocation' was not used when Marks joined the industry 55 years earlier; 60/40 was standard.
- From 2009 through 2021 expected returns on debt were extremely low and far below historical equity returns.
- In an efficient market all ways of reaching a given risk level produce the same expected return.

## Quotes

> at bottom, there are only two asset classes: ownership and debt.

> This isn’t a difference in degree; it’s a difference in kind.

> You can’t simultaneously emphasize both preservation of capital and maximization of growth, or defense and offense.
