# Uncertainty II

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** May 28, 2020 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/uncertainty-ii.pdf)

## Summary

A post-script to the memo Uncertainty, written during the Covid-19 lockdowns. Marks uses Mark Lilla's New York Times article 'No One Knows What's Going to Happen' to restate that the future is unknowable and path-dependent: it is created by collective decisions not yet made, so forecasts are right only if nothing changes in between. He adds that people without expertise cannot tell who the real experts are, that expertise and predictive ability are different things, and that judgment, not expertise, must settle trade-offs such as reopening the economy versus minimising deaths. Via Morgan Housel he stresses the tail-end consequences of risk, and he closes with his 2001 idea that one cannot predict but can prepare by becoming more cautious when markets are vulnerable.

## Key Ideas

1. The future does not yet exist; it is path-dependent on decisions, behaviour and luck, which makes forecasting futile.
2. People hunger for authoritative forecasts and, when a prophet fails, anoint a new one rather than conclude forecasting doesn't work.
3. You may need to be an expert to identify true experts; lay people cannot vet expertise.
4. Common errors: confusing intelligence with field knowledge, knowledge with insight, expertise with predictive ability, and success with universal wisdom.
5. Trade-offs like reopening versus infection control are matters of judgment, with no algorithm to resolve them.
6. Risk has three sides (odds, average consequences, tail-end consequences); the tail is what matters and is learned through experience.
7. Past worst cases are the only guide yet need not bound future severity.
8. Preparation without prediction: become more cautious when economic developments and investor behaviour make markets vulnerable.

## Market Context

Late May 2020, during the Covid-19 pandemic: debates over how fast to reopen the U.S. economy, mass job losses (36 million in two months per Housel), an upcoming November presidential election, and a flood of conflicting expert opinions.

## Notable Claims

- Most financial history sits within two standard deviations, but the interesting events lie outside.
- Housel: 36 million people lost their jobs in two months because of the virus.
- A legal scholar predicted in mid-March 2020 that no more than 500 Americans would die of Covid-19.

## Quotes

> That's the catch: you may have to be an expert in a field in order to be able to figure out who the true experts are.

> They never say, "I guess forecasting doesn't work."

> we may not be able to predict the future, but that doesn't mean we're powerless to deal with it.
