# First Quarter Performance

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** April 11, 1991 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1991-04-11-first-quarter-client-performance.pdf)

## Summary

A short client letter, sent by Marks from Trust Company of the West, that introduces the pendulum metaphor for market psychology. Marks argues that markets rarely sit at the midpoint between euphoria and depression and that the move toward an extreme supplies the energy for the swing back. He applies this to the late-1990 trough, when fear about the economy and Iraq left non-gilt-edge securities deeply undervalued, and to the sharp first-quarter 1991 rebound in his high yield, convertible and distressed-debt portfolios. He closes by saying prices are now nearer fair than excessive, that the bargains of late 1990 are gone, and that clients should not expect the quarter's returns to continue.

## Key Ideas

1. Market psychology moves like a pendulum and spends little time at the midpoint of its arc.
2. The movement toward an extreme itself creates the force that later pulls prices back toward the middle.
3. The swing runs between euphoria and depression, and so between overpriced and underpriced.
4. Late 1990 was a negative extreme: fear about the economy and Iraq caused excessive risk aversion and undervalued lower-quality securities.
5. Predicting each swing is unrealistic; the sensible approach is to notice extremes, adjust slightly, and refuse to follow the herd.
6. After a rapid rebound, the investor should stop acting as if bargains are still plentiful and lower return expectations.

## Market Context

Written after the late-1990 sell-off driven by recession fears and the Iraq conflict, and after a strong first-quarter 1991 rally in high yield bonds, convertibles and distressed debt. Marks notes the rally came without great improvement in the fundamental economy.

## Notable Claims

- High yield bonds had their best quarter since Q2 1980.
- Convertibles had their best quarter since Q4 1982.
- Distressed-debt Special Credits portfolios outperformed both high yield and convertibles.
- Late-1990 markets were at a negative extreme.
- Prices after the rebound are closer to fair.

## Quotes

> In fact, it is the movement toward an extreme itself that supplies the energy for the swing back.

> This oscillation is one of the most dependable features of the investment world, and investor psychology seems to spend much more time at the extremes than it does at a "happy medium."
