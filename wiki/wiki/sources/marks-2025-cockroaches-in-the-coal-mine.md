# Cockroaches in the Coal Mine

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** November 6, 2025 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/cockroaches-in-the-coal-mine.pdf)

## Summary

Marks responds to a run of sub-investment grade credit failures in autumn 2025. These include the bankruptcies of First Brands and Tricolor, fraud disclosures at Zions and Western Alliance, and the collapse of Broadband Telecom and Bridgevoice on fabricated receivables. Jamie Dimon had warned that one cockroach usually means more. Marks argues these cases do not indict private credit or high yield as a whole, because defaults of more than 2% a year are normal. They are a reminder that spreads exist to pay for credit risk. He explains the cycle in attitudes toward risk: good times breed complacency and lower standards, and bad times expose the malinvestment. He links this to Galbraith's "bezzle", undiscovered fraud that grows in booms and shrinks in busts. He calls the problem systematic, a recurring behavioral pattern, but not systemic, since the financial plumbing is sound. He then describes how Oaktree's mosaic-style research flagged First Brands before it filed, and he lists lessons on credit discipline and the value of exiting public debt.

## Key Ideas

1. Markets obsess over one topic at a time; in late 2025 it was a string of sub-investment grade credit episodes.
2. Private credit grew from about 2011 with perhaps $2 trillion of inflows; competition eroded lenders' early advantages, and the sector had not been tested by a downturn.
3. Security prices swing more than intrinsic values mainly because attitudes toward risk swing between risk tolerance and risk aversion.
4. Booms produce malinvestment and fraud (the 'bezzle'), which busts reveal; the worst loans are made in the best of times.
5. Clustered frauds are systematic (behavioral and recurring) but not systemic (no flaw in the plumbing that spreads failure).
6. Credit analysis builds a mosaic of inferences toward a preponderance of evidence; scale spreads research cost and gives more points of contact.
7. The payoff in spotting credit defects comes from being early; liquid public debt can be exited more readily than private credit.

## Market Context

Autumn 2025, after about sixteen years of largely uninterrupted growth, rising markets and profitable risk-taking. Private credit had become the most asked-about sector. First Brands filed for bankruptcy in late September, and regional banks disclosed borrower frauds in mid-October, which hit alternative asset manager stocks.

## Notable Claims

- More than 2% of high yield bonds by value default in a typical year.
- About $2 trillion flowed into private credit after 2011.
- First Brands' obligations were $11.6 billion versus $5.9 billion of disclosed debt.
- Zions took a $50 million write-down on loans to two borrowers (quoted from the FT).
- Alternative asset manager stocks fell 5-7% on October 16.

## Quotes

> Rather, it’s just a reminder that the yield spreads people care about so much are there for a reason: because sub-investment grade debt entails credit risk.

> In heady times, rather than say, “That’s too good to be true,” people are more likely to ask, “How can I get in on that?”

> So, it isn’t ‘‘systemic,’’ but it is “systematic.”
