# The Calculus of Value

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** August 13, 2025 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/the-calculus-of-value.pdf)

## Summary

Marks sets out, for the first time in this form, where investment value comes from and how price relates to it, then applies the framework to US stocks in mid-2025. Value is subjective and derives from an asset's present and future earning power; assets without earning power, such as art or gold, cannot be valued intrinsically. Price is the consensus result of a daily tug-of-war between optimists and pessimists, so it swings more than value. Value exerts a 'magnetic' pull on price, but only reliably in the long run, and superior returns come from anticipating changes in value or in the price-value gap better than others. Applying this, he finds fundamentals weaker than at the start of 2025 after the April tariffs, yet prices higher, with record price-to-sales, record market cap to GDP, a Barclays euphoria gauge at bubble levels and near-record tight credit spreads. He moves from 'elevated' to 'worrisome', recommends 'INVESTCON 5' (shift from aggressive to defensive holdings), and suggests credit as a defensive holding.

## Key Ideas

1. An asset's intrinsic value comes from its current and future earning power; non-earning assets cannot be valued intrinsically.
2. Market price is the consensus of optimists and pessimists and is driven largely by psychology, so it moves more than value.
3. Value exerts a magnetic pull on price, but only 'more likely' and in the long run; betting heavily on fast convergence is dangerous.
4. Long-run returns depend on being right about earning power; short-run returns depend mostly on changes in price.
5. Superior risk-adjusted returns come from changes in value or in the price-value relationship that others did not anticipate.
6. In 2025 the concern was the 22 P/E on the 493 non-Magnificent-Seven stocks, more than the roughly 33 P/E on the Seven.
7. 'It's different this time' needs scrutiny, but missing real change is also costly; with AI, it may be different for some companies.
8. Defensive positioning comes in graded steps (INVESTCONs); overvaluation never means 'sure to fall soon', so extreme steps are rarely justified.

## Market Context

August 2025. US stocks fell up to 10% in Q1, dropped further after the April 2 tariff announcement (S&P 500 15% below end-2024, ten-year Treasury yield up to 4½%), then rallied 29% from April 8 on tariff relief, a corporate-friendly tax bill and AI enthusiasm. Valuation indicators were at records, credit spreads were near all-time tights, and meme stocks had returned.

## Notable Claims

- Buying the S&P 500 at 23x forward earnings (1987-2014) gave +2% to -2% ten-year annual returns (J.P. Morgan).
- S&P 500 price-to-sales at an all-time high (FT/Bloomberg).
- S&P 500 rose 29% from its April 8 low.
- Non-Magnificent-493 trade at a P/E of 22.
- Magnificent Seven are about one-third of S&P 500 value and drove over half its 2023-24 return.
