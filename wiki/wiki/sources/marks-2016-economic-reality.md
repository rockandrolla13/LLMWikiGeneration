# Economic Reality

**Author:** Howard Marks · **Date:** May 26, 2016 · **Type:** memo (article) · **Original:** [oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/economic-reality.pdf)

## Summary

Written during the 2016 US election campaign, this memo argues that politicians routinely promise things that economic laws do not allow, replacing 'or' with 'and'. Marks works through examples: central banks can bring future activity forward but cannot raise an economy's natural growth rate; easy construction finance produced empty homes in China, Ireland, Spain and Arizona; state-owned lenders funding state-owned borrowers make uneconomic decisions; taxpayers are mobile, so higher top rates do not raise proportional revenue; printing money debases the currency, as in Weimar Germany; and not every country can devalue at once. He spends the most space on globalisation and the 'bring back the jobs' promise, showing manufacturing output near an all-time high while employment fell, and that tariffs would raise consumer prices. He ends with lists of what governments can and cannot do and a Churchill quote about lifting oneself by a bucket handle.

## Key Ideas

1. Economics is the study of choice under scarcity, but politicians promise outcomes as if no trade-offs exist.
2. Central banks can accelerate activity and prevent meltdowns, but long-run output depends on labour and productivity.
3. Successive stimulus doses since the crisis have produced less growth than hoped, with diminishing impact.
4. When lenders and borrowers do not bear losses (state banks lending to state firms), bad economic decisions follow.
5. Taxpayers are mobile, so raising top rates by 10% does not raise 10% more revenue.
6. Inflation is largely psychological: the US could not stop it in the 1970s and nations cannot start it now.
7. US manufacturing jobs were lost mainly to productivity, not trade; tariffs would protect some jobs at the cost of higher prices for everyone.
8. Governments mostly redistribute wealth and shift it across time; only fundamental reforms raise long-run growth.

## Market Context

May 2016, during the US presidential primaries, with populist candidates Donald Trump and Bernie Sanders gaining support. Seven years of central bank stimulus had produced weak growth and very low inflation. Policy proposals included large tariffs on China and Mexico, a $15 minimum wage, free college and healthcare, and breaking up big banks.

## Notable Claims

- US manufacturing employment down 37% from 1979 peak while output is near a high.
- 3.2 million jobs lost to China since 2001, far fewer than to productivity.
- Ford vs foreign-plant labour cost gap in 2005 (citing Economic Policy Institute).
- California's top earners paid about 19% of state income tax (NYT).
- Foreign brands exceed 55% of US car sales.

## Quotes

> in politics and government – unlike the real world – the word “or” often goes out the window, replaced by “and.” No choices are necessary.

> Here’s a shocker: you can set prices for goods, but you can’t make people produce them.

> Perhaps if the government wants to preserve jobs it should just outlaw productivity gains.
