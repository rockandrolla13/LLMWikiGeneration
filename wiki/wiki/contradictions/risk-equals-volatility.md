# Is Risk the Same as Volatility?

**Status:** unresolved

## Claim A: volatility measures risk

The wiki's [[concepts/volatility-targeting|Volatility Targeting]] page defines the technique as scaling exposure inversely to estimated price volatility "so risk stays roughly constant". That wording treats volatility as the risk being held constant. The page draws on [[sources/carver-2015-systematic-trading|Systematic Trading]] (2015) and [[sources/israel-2017-common-factors-corporate-bond-returns|Common Factors in Corporate Bond Returns]] (2017). [[concepts/risk-parity|Risk Parity]] also allocates "risk" using volatility.

## Claim B: risk is the chance of losing money, and volatility is not it

In [[sources/marks-2001-whats-it-all-about-alpha|What's It All About, Alpha?]] (July 2001), Howard Marks writes that he does not think risk is synonymous with volatility. Oaktree means by risk the chance of losing its clients' money, and he says the firm does not think about volatility. He also says this kind of risk cannot be quantified, even after the fact. He argues that academics adopted volatility as the measure of risk because it is absolute and quantifiable. In [[sources/marks-2001-safety-first-but-where|Safety First . . . But Where?]] (April 2001) he calls volatility "the theoretical definition of risk". He then says forced selling at the bottom can turn it into permanent loss. In [[sources/marks-2006-risk|Risk]] (January 2006) he returns to the point at length. He writes that academics settled on volatility as the proxy for risk "as a matter of convenience", because they needed a number that was objective. He says risk is "first and foremost" the likelihood of losing money. See [[concepts/risk-vs-volatility|Risk Is Not Volatility]].

## Where exactly they conflict

Both claims are about what "risk" means for a portfolio. Claim A holds a portfolio's risk steady by holding its volatility steady. Claim B denies that holding volatility steady holds risk steady, because the loss that matters is permanent and asset-specific.

## Points that bear on a resolution

These are editorial observations. Neither source offers them as a resolution.

- Claim A may use volatility as a working proxy rather than as a definition. The concept page's wording does not say so. The Carver source page describes targeting a chosen annualised volatility without saying whether volatility is the definition of risk or a proxy for it.
- Marks ties his view to capital in locked-up funds or long-term relationships. A mark-to-market portfolio that must meet margin or redemptions faces volatility as a cost in its own right. That cost is the forced-selling route Marks himself describes.
- The two can coexist in practice: a volatility target bounds short-run swings, while it says nothing about default or recovery risk in a single credit.

## What would settle it

A source that compares volatility with realised permanent loss across asset classes, such as high yield and distressed debt, over full cycles. The wiki has no such source yet.
