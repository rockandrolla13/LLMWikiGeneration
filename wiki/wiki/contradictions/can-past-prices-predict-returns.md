# Can Past Price Moves Predict Future Returns?

**Status:** unresolved

## Claim A: past winners keep winning over intermediate horizons

The wiki's [[concepts/momentum-trend-following|Momentum and Trend Following]] page defines momentum as the tendency of recent winners to keep outperforming recent losers over intermediate horizons. It says simple momentum and trend-following are profitable across asset classes. The page draws on [[sources/ilmanen-2011-expected-returns|Expected Returns]] (2011), which lists momentum with carry and value as strategies that earn premia across several asset classes. It also draws on [[sources/lee-2021-cds-momentum-cross-market-spillovers|CDS Momentum]] (2021), which finds momentum in single-name CDS returns that bond and equity factors do not explain.

## Claim B: price movements carry no useful message about future prices

In [[sources/marks-2016-what-does-the-market-know|What Does the Market Know?]] (January 2016), Howard Marks says the market "has no special insight and conveys no consistently helpful message". He describes investors who try to sell on declines that will continue and buy dips that will recover. If they think they can tell which is which "from watching the market movements themselves", he says, then "we – again – have a fundamental disagreement". He writes that future price movements can only be predicted from the relationship between price and fundamentals, and only in the long term. He ends: "The market has nothing useful to contribute on this subject."

## Where exactly they conflict

Both claims are about whether past price moves, on their own, help predict future returns. Claim A says they do, at intermediate horizons and across asset classes. Claim B says they do not, and that only price relative to fundamentals predicts returns.

## Points that bear on a resolution

These are editorial observations. Neither source offers them as a resolution.

- Marks writes about reacting to a sharp market decline, in January 2016. The momentum evidence concerns systematic rules applied across many assets. Marks does not discuss systematic momentum or trend-following rules in this memo.
- Marks says "consistently helpful". A premium that holds on average across many bets but fails often in single cases might fit both claims.
- The CDS result is cross-sectional: it ranks firms against each other. Marks speaks of the direction of the market as a whole.

## What would settle it

An out-of-sample test of trend signals on market-level returns after sharp declines, net of costs, compared with value signals over the same periods. The wiki has no such source yet.
