# Edward Chancellor

## Notes

Person named in 6 of the 165 Howard Marks memos in the collection.

Context from the memos:

- [[sources/marks-2000-bubble-com|bubble.com]] (2000): Writing at the very start of 2000, Marks compares the technology, Internet and telecom boom with the 1720 South Sea Bubble as told in Edward Chancellor's 'Devil Take the Hindmost'.
- [[sources/marks-2024-easy-money|Easy Money]] (2024): Prompted by Edward Chancellor's book The Price of Time, Marks extends his 'Sea Change' thesis by cataloguing ten ways that declining and ultra-low interest rates distort behaviour: they stimulate the economy, erase perceived opportunity costs, lift asset prices, push 'handcuff volunteers' into risk and Hayekian 'malinvestment', make weak deals easy to finance, encourage fragile leverage, tempt borrowing short to lend long, create expectations that rates will stay low, transfer wealth from savers to borrowers, and lay the groundwork for the next crisis.
